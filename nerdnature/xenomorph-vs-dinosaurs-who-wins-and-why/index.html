Xenomorph vs Dinosaurs: Who Wins and Why

A starving xenomorph fights Deinonychus, Allosaurus, Diplodocus and T. rex in a wound-carrying gauntlet to see which dinosaur, if any, can stop it.

T. rex ends the xenomorph with one bite, after the xenomorph beat three dinosaurs.

Every NerdNature fight

The Raptor in the Ferns

F

XenomorphXenomorph vs Deinonychus

The xenomorph wins because it reads the raptor's opening trick and then uses it. Deinonychus darts in on the left with jaws wide and a lot of noise. The xenomorph flinches, and that flinch is the bait. The real attack is supposed to come from the other side. The xenomorph has no eyes, but, going by the comic Alien: Paradiso, it senses the electrical activity inside a living body and the scent trail of panic. It takes the lesson from the feint and lunges first.

The sickle claw is the raptor's famous weapon, but it isn't a slasher. A robotic Deinonychus leg barely punched pig skin and bounced off crocodile hide. Research comparing the foot to hawks and eagles points to a pinning tool: land on the prey, hold it with body weight, hook in and bite. So when the raptor lands on the xenomorph's back, its claws skid across the black shell. They are built for hide, not plate.

The raptor's one real threat is the bite. It can reach about 8,200 Newtons, close to a ton of force and alligator territory, and it goes for the base of the neck. For a second you'd bet on the raptor. Then the barbed tail comes around past the xenomorph's hip and goes straight through the raptor as it shifts its grip. The inner jaw punches through the skull.

Deinonychus gets F tier because nothing it carries gets through that shell. Its pack is mostly movie as well. Tooth chemistry shows young and adult Deinonychus ate different food, so it hunts more like a Komodo dragon free-for-all than a wolf pack. It has no backup, and its claws and teeth aren't enough.

The Allosaurus Ambush

B

XenomorphXenomorph vs Allosaurus

Allosaurus, 30 feet long and about two tons, follows the raptor blood on the xenomorph's teeth and closes the only exit. The xenomorph's claws split the skin on its leg, but the leg doesn't buckle. Then the head strike lands. A 2001 study found a skull that could take about 55,500 Newtons on the teeth, roughly six tons, though weak jaw muscles. Later neck-muscle work says it works more like a falcon than an axe: drive the teeth down, then rip back and up.

The strike throws the xenomorph into a tree and cracks its shell like a fault line in ice. Then the jaws close and the teeth find the seams between the plates. Recent estimates put this bite near 9,400 Newtons, about a ton of force. That is weak for a 30-foot predator, but it is enough to splinter plates one by one as the Allosaurus lifts the xenomorph clear.

What turns it is the acid. The drool burned nothing, which matches Alien 3, where slime drips on Ripley's face and she is fine. The acid is in the blood. In the first Alien film, blood from one cut facehugger leg ate down through several decks of the ship. Now that blood is in the Allosaurus's mouth. The gums burn and the jaw flinches open.

The xenomorph goes toward the dinosaur, rakes its back, then drives the barbed tail up through the throat and the roof of the mouth. The acid keeps eating through gums, tongue and throat, and the knees fold. B tier is for a true hunter that nearly won. It cost the xenomorph a piece of its shell.

The Wall of Mass

C

XenomorphXenomorph vs Diplodocus herd

The problem here is size. Each Diplodocus is about 80 feet long and 13 to 16 tons. The xenomorph's claws skid off a back leg like nails on a boulder, and its tail bounces off the same leg. The famous Diplodocus sonic whip doesn't save the herd either. That idea came from a 1997 computer model. A 2022 digital rebuild of the tail found it could only be kept from wrecking itself by cutting the speed well down, so it never reaches the speed of sound a sonic boom needs. The tail slaps hard but doesn't crack.

The xenomorph has one plan left, which is to climb. Sauropod neck bones are hollowed out by air sacs, and the brain stem sits behind a lightly built joint where the neck meets the skull. The first climb fails. The thin end of a different animal's tail hits the xenomorph, and it skids 30 feet through the mud. More plates crack, and the herd's feet slam down around it.

The second climb works. The xenomorph is down to one good arm and both feet, with no caution left. It hauls itself up the neck, braces on the vertebrae, and its inner jaw punches through the base of the skull. The front legs fold and the Diplodocus settles into the mud as the herd thunders away.

Diplodocus gets C tier because nothing here could touch the xenomorph by size alone. It took exactly one point of failure, the soft joint at the skull. Every hit the xenomorph took in this fight is also a debt it carries into the next one.

The King at the Tree Line

S

Tyrannosaurus rexXenomorph vs Tyrannosaurus rex

T. rex is 40 feet long and about 9 tons. Holding still, as in Jurassic Park, doesn't work. A 2006 study found its forward-facing eyes overlap across 55 degrees, more than a hawk's. It runs around 12 mph by 2017 estimates, but three strides close the gap. The xenomorph dodges, and the first bite splits a tree trunk with a crack like a rifle shot.

The bite is the number the fight hangs on. It puts 35,000 to 57,000 Newtons through a single back tooth, 4 to 6 tons of force. That is up to six times the Allosaurus bite that splintered the shell. The next bite finds the tail, and the barbed length crumbles. The acid floods T. rex's mouth, and the neck, which modeling says could fling a 100-pound body more than 15 feet, tosses the xenomorph into the mud.

The xenomorph then uses everything it has learned. In Alien Resurrection, xenomorphs kill one of their own so its blood will melt through a cage. So it runs the raptor's trick: a fake lunge at the burned, open jaws. The head flinches back an inch, because a burned mouth flinches easily. The xenomorph gets under the chin and its inner jaw punches into the throat, with acid running into the wound.

But the debt comes due. The xenomorph is hanging on to a thrashing nine-ton animal with one working arm and no tail to brace with. It holds through two swings and peels off on the third. T. rex doesn't need to outthink anything. It needs to land once. The jaws close across the torso with four to six tons on a shell that split under one, and this time the shell caves.

That one bite earns T. rex S tier. It is the only dinosaur the xenomorph couldn't get through, because the bite never needed a second chance. T. rex is left smoking and staggering, with a hole in its throat, but it stays on its feet.

What it comes down to

The xenomorph beats three dinosaurs because it learns: it turns the raptor's feint against it, uses its acid blood against the Allosaurus, and finds the Diplodocus's weak neck joint. It also beats T. rex's guard. But every wound carries over. By the last fight it has a cracked shell, a ruined tail and one working arm. T. rex doesn't have to out-think it, only land one bite of four to six tons on a shell that already broke under one. T. rex may not live through the night with acid in its mouth.
