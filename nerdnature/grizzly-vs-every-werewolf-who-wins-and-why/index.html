Grizzly vs Every Werewolf: Who Wins and Why

A 600-pound grizzly with no silver and no magic fights folklore, Norse, tabletop and Teen Wolf werewolves back to back, and each fight costs it something.

The grizzly survives all four, barely: overall tier B.

Every NerdNature fight

The Folklore Wolfman

A

Grizzly bearGrizzly bear vs the folklore Wolf Man

This werewolf is a man's frame under fur. The 1941 film The Wolf Man used a 6-ft-2 actor in makeup, so the bear outweighs it about three to one. We also strip out the movie's silver rule. Old European folklore had no silver rule, and those werewolves bled. With no silver in the forest and no healing, this is a heavy, tough animal against a fragile body.

The Wolfman does land the first hit. It is lighter and quicker, and it rakes the bear's shoulder in four lines. The hit fails to stop the bear because it lands on the thickest skin on the animal. Grizzly hide is heaviest over the neck and shoulders, where bears aim at each other. In a fall coat it also sits under a long, dense layer of guard hairs. A slash that would gut a man stops short of anything vital.

Then the bear fights its own way. It lunges and bites the Wolfman's forearm. Grizzly bite force is near 1,000 psi, though most such figures are estimates. The arm folds. The Wolfman is standing tall on two legs, and that high center of gravity is a liability against a low, wide animal. The bear slams into its chest and puts it on its back.

Once the Wolfman is down, the long reach is useless. 600 pounds sits on top of it, claws scrape at a flank, and the bear finds the throat. Speed and reach lose to mass and bite as soon as the fight hits the ground. The tier is A, but the bear pays for it. The shoulder is torn open and stiffening in the cold, and the energy it burned doesn't come back tonight.

The Berserker Beast

B

Grizzly bearGrizzly bear vs the Norse berserker

This werewolf is the Norse legend of the Ulfhednar and berserkers with the wolf skin grown on. The sagas don't undersell them. The Ynglinga saga says Odin's warriors bit their shields, were as strong as bears or wild bulls, and couldn't be hurt by fire or iron. The saga's list has no teeth in it. The berserker hits the already-hurt bear like a thrown boulder, drives it into a spruce, and cracks ribs.

The berserker's real weakness is that it feels nothing. A bite works because pain makes an animal flinch, stop or run. The bear bites it in the side, the same bite that crushed a forearm earlier, and the berserker keeps swinging as if the wound belonged to someone else. So the bear stops trading blows. It tucks its chin and covers its throat behind a column of neck muscle and a skull built for fights with other bears.

That works because of how muscle burns fuel. The first energy tank runs out in about 10 seconds of all-out effort and the second in up to two minutes. The last is too slow to support haymakers. A normal fighter feels that wall and paces himself. The berserker can't feel it, so it throws every punch like the first one.

The sagas recorded this crash. Egil's saga says nothing could withstand these men while the fit lasted, and that afterward they were weaker than usual. The blows soften, the howl cracks, and each lunge arrives a half second late. The bear rises and closes its jaws on the throat. The tier is B because the win is built on outlasting, which is fragile. A fresh bear wins comfortably, but this one finishes with cracked ribs.

The Regenerator

C

Grizzly bearGrizzly bear vs the regenerator

This werewolf heals the way Werewolf: The Apocalypse werewolves do. In the tabletop game they close a wound every combat turn. The damage that sticks comes from silver, fire and the attacks of other supernatural creatures. The Witcher 3 uses the same playbook. Nobody in the forest has silver or fire. We also give this werewolf a body the size of the others, not the game's 9-ft war form.

On open ground the bear is losing. It tears a mouthful from the shoulder and the wound zips shut. Flank, forearm and ribs all seal behind the teeth, while every swipe the Regenerator lands stays a wound. One side spends a finite body and the other refills its account. A claw rakes the bear's face, and the bear loses slowly, like a tide taking a beach.

The bear's answer is to stop tearing, because torn flesh is what a regenerator fixes best. It lunges low and carries both of them into the deepest part of the creek. It clamps its forelimbs on the neck and shoulders and pushes the head under. The shoulder hump drives the forelimbs down like pistons with 600 pounds riding on top, and a body pinned underwater has nothing to push against.

Healing fixes wounds, and drowning isn't a wound. It's an empty tank. Most people underwater black out in about three minutes, and the brain starts to suffer around five. Thrashing burns oxygen even faster. No canon settles whether a regenerator can drown, so this is our own call on biology. The bear's forepaw is bitten through, and on dry ground it would lose. The tier is C, because the creek gave the bear its weapon.

The Thinking Wolf

D

Grizzly bearGrizzly bear vs the Teen Wolf-style thinking wolf

This chapter needs a werewolf that keeps a human mind. We rule out two other franchises. Harry Potter werewolves lose their minds completely. Twilight's wolves keep theirs but are far bigger than ordinary wolves, and the books admit they're shapeshifters. The fit is Teen Wolf. It has yellow eyes, a human mind in control, claws, superhuman strength and healing. Small wounds close in moments, big ones in hours, and a killing wound still kills. Peter Hale went down to fire and a slash across the throat.

The thinking wolf wins the opening. It circles outside paw range and watches the bear's bad side until it knows where the bear flinches. It herds the bear onto loose scree with a rock face behind it. After the bear's one swipe, the cuts close within a minute. Then it fakes left, slides right, and rips the bitten forepaw. The bear goes down on three limbs with its back against stone.

The bear's last move is not a plan. It drags itself into a cleft just wide enough for its bulk, so stone covers both flanks and its back and there is only one way in. Staying still slows the forepaw bleed to a seep. A grizzly can sit in a den for months without eating or drinking. Now time favors the bear, and the thinking wolf will never get a weaker one, so it has to come through the front.

Its lunge hits a hard limit. A human brain needs a measurable fraction of a second to see and react. Superhuman reflexes shrink that gap but don't erase it, and a body already launched forward is cargo. The bear's jaws close across its face with the thousand-pound-per-square-inch bite. The thinking wolf measured a limping bear and missed how far 600 pounds can throw its head from a crouch. A cut throat finished Peter Hale, and a crushed skull is worse. The tier is D, and a rematch probably kills the bear.

What it comes down to

The grizzly survives all four werewolves, but each win costs more, and we score the night overall tier B. The bear won by dragging every fight onto its own terms: the ground, its armor, the creek, and the rock. The folklore Wolfman dies to mass and bite. The berserker burns out. The regenerator drowns, which is our own call. The thinking wolf is the only rulebook that nearly beats a bear. It has a human brain, and the bear won by a few inches of reach it never measured.
