300 Spartans vs Mordor, White Walkers, Fire Nation

We send the Spartan phalanx of Thermopylae against four fantasy armies, one assault each, with every man lost staying lost, and score each fight S to F.

Not as an army: overall tier F, with 11 Spartans left standing.

Every NerdHistory fight

The Unsullied

C

Spartans survive the day; the Unsullied win a fight to the finish300 Spartans vs 3,000 Unsullied

This is the fairest fight on the card, and the Spartans start it with the better kit. The Unsullied carry a spear, shield, short sword, quilted tunic and spiked bronze cap, and any real armor is the buyer's problem. The Spartans wear bronze. We fight it where the ground narrows, so the Unsullied can't spread out and use their 10-to-1 edge.

The Spartan dory is a long thrusting spear, and it reaches past the first rank. Every thrust goes over a shield rim into a throat, an armpit or the gap above a knee, and against quilted cloth it doesn't have to be perfect. The Unsullied don't flinch from a spear through the thigh, since they are trained to shrug off pain, but enduring pain isn't the same as being tireless.

The shove decides it. A few hours of pushing drains the legs. The real 300 never fought alone, because other Greeks held the pass with them. Here nobody relieves them, while the Unsullied rotate fresh men forward every few minutes.

The math backs that up. At Qohor in the books, 3,000 Unsullied took 18 Dothraki charges, killed 12,000 men and lost 2,400, about five to one. Give each Spartan five kills and 1,500 Unsullied are still standing when the last one falls. We score it a C because the Spartans only hold until dusk, when the assault ends by rule, with about 100 of them down.

The Orcs of Mordor

D

Spartans survive the night, bent but unbrokenSpartan phalanx vs Orcs, Uruk-hai and an Olog-hai troll

Spears do well early. They punch over the shield rims into faces, and the front ranks of orcs drop in heaps. But an orc with a spear in the belly keeps swinging, and the Uruk-hai in black armor lean into a blade and shove. In Tolkien's books even a huge orc chieftain is only almost man-high, so numbers and stubbornness do the work, not size. The shields that held all day start sliding backward.

The troll is what bends the line. Tolkien's Olog-hai are Sauron's own breed and can stand the sun while his will holds them. We call it 12 feet. Say it weighs 3 tons and runs 10 miles an hour. That's about 27,000 joules, and stopping it inside a foot, the depth of a shield and a chest, means roughly 10 tons of average force.

A shield is a wooden board skinned in thin bronze, so it explodes. The man who takes the hit is pinned against his own rank and has nowhere for the energy to go but his chest. A whole file flies back 10 feet and the line bows inward at the middle, a shape no phalanx is supposed to take.

The line still doesn't snap, and the reason is what the Spartans do next. The second and third ranks step over the dead and lock shields. They don't stab the hide, because in the books Boromir's sword rings off a troll's arm and is notched. Three spears go low into the backs of its knees, it drops, and two spears go in under the jaw. It falls on the front rank, the way Pippin's troll falls on him at the Black Gate. We score it a D: the formation holds, but it never recovers its shape. At first light the orcs retreat from the sun, with about 120 Spartans left.

The White Walkers

F

White Walkers (Spartans survive only on the one-assault rule)Spartan phalanx vs White Walkers and wights

The spear is a weapon built to make a living body stop working. It cuts blood flow or collapses a lung. A wight has no blood to lose and no lungs to collapse, so stabbing one is like stabbing a coat. One keeps walking up the shaft with the iron head sticking out of its back. The Spartans switch to the xiphos and chop arms and legs, which is their best stretch of the fight.

It still isn't enough. In A Game of Thrones, after Jon Snow cuts off a wight's arm, the severed hand still claws at his calf, and only fire finally destroys the wight. The Spartans have no fire, and a phalanx needs a kill every second, not one every ten. Then three White Walkers walk out of the snow. In the books they're the Others, and an Other's blade touches the spearhead and the iron shatters.

The cold explains why. In the first book an Other's blade shatters Ser Waymar Royce's steel sword into shards. Bronze is mostly copper, which is generally said to keep its toughness in the cold, while iron and steel turn brittle. The spearhead is iron, so it breaks. The bronze butt spike, the sauroter, doesn't, and the Spartan drives it into a Walker's chest. It's the best thrust he's thrown in two days, and it doesn't matter, because only dragonglass and Valyrian steel are known to kill a Walker.

Dragonglass is obsidian, and nobody packed any. Then the dead turn on them. A phalanx runs on every man trusting the shield beside him, and when the man next to a hoplite rises with ice-blue eyes, the hole opens from inside. We score it an F. The Spartans live only because each army gets one assault, and about 80 die, leaving 40.

The Fire Nation

F

Fire Nation40 Spartans vs Fire Nation war balloons, catapults and firebenders

Nothing here comes within spear range. Four war balloons, catapults on the ridge and rows of firebenders in crimson armor stay a long way off. The balloons are the Fire Nation airships from Avatar: The Last Airbender, each flown by firebenders, one who keeps the heat up and one who fights. Burning pitch pots and ribbons of flame roll over the wall while the balloons drop fire where a raised shield can't cover.

The Spartans do what they've always done and lock up tighter, which is the mistake. A phalanx absorbs a blow by pressing closer, and under fire that packs every man into a heat trap. If they break, benders pick off stragglers. If they advance, the benders back up, and the catapults sit a quarter mile behind them. A formation built to win at arm's length becomes an oven with 40 men inside.

The bronze is part of the problem. A hoplite's helmet, greaves and often the breastplate are bronze worn close to the skin, and thin metal in flame heats fast. The armor stops protecting the man and starts cooking him, so men drop from heat, not wounds.

The man with the red strip makes the one smart play left. His spear has no head, only the bronze spike. He ignores the bender charging the blast and throws at the one keeping the balloon aloft. The flame dies with him, the basket lurches, and the balloon sags into its neighbor, which buys about 40 seconds. Then fire comes in from the other side. We score it an F because the phalanx can't touch what's killing it. Thermopylae was a fight at arm's length, but here nobody gets close enough. Eleven Spartans are still standing.

What it comes down to

No, 300 Spartans can't survive all four armies as an army: the overall tier is F, with 11 men standing in the ash. The biggest army, tens of thousands of orcs, never broke the line. A phalanx is built for an enemy that walks up to it and stays dead when it falls. The White Walkers break the second rule by raising the dead, and the Fire Nation breaks the first by never coming into reach. The Unsullied would have won a fight to the finish, but the one-assault rule leaves the Spartans alive to meet the others.
