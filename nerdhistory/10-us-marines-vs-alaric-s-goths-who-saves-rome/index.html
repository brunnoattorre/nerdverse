10 US Marines vs Alaric's Goths: Who Saves Rome?

Ten US Marines with 2,100 rounds and no resupply hold Rome against Alaric's Goths on August 24, 410, and we grade the city's survival at every stand.

The Marines break the Goths' will, not their numbers, and Rome survives the night.

The first wave breaks on the northern road

C

The Marines hold the gate10 US Marines vs Alaric's Goths at the Salarian Gate

The Marines win the gate because they only have to defend one door. The Goths never smashed through Rome's walls in 410. Procopius, writing more than a century later, says the Goths got in when people inside opened the Salarian Gate. So the sergeant isn't defending 12 miles of wall. He puts all ten Marines in the Salarian Gate house, where the old salt road enters from the north.

Ammunition decides how they shoot. 2,100 rounds is 210 a man, so nobody sprays. Every shot is single and aimed, and nobody fires until the lead rank passes a roadside tomb 100 yards out, where every round lands. The cost is time, because a running man covers 100 yards in a handful of seconds. When the rifles crack together, the front rank goes down in a line.

The bullet is why shields don't matter. A 5.56mm round is less than a quarter inch across and leaves the muzzle at about 3,000 feet per second, which matches the roughly 3,020 feet per second spec for M855 ball. A wooden shield barely slows it, and even mail won't stop it. Inside a body it tumbles. The sergeant never has to wait for a gap in the shield wall, because he shoots straight through it. Nobody alive in 410 has heard a gunshot, so the column also stalls for most of a minute.

The price comes when a dozen Goths get a ladder onto the wall beside the gate house. The 100-yard rule has a cost, and the fight becomes a brawl at three feet. A thrown axe cracks one Marine's ceramic plate, and the plate holds. The spear that follows comes in low, under the plate's bottom edge, and kills him. The Goths on the wall last about ten more seconds, and the war bands pull back. We score it a C. The gate held, but 1,400 rounds are left and Alaric hasn't really started.

Fire on the Tiber

F

The Marines hold the bridge9 US Marines vs Alaric's Goths at the stone bridge over the Tiber

Alaric answers by going around. This is his third siege of Rome in two years. The first time he took the river, starved the city and let it buy him off with gold, silver and 3,000 pounds of pepper. He can count: ten strange men can stand in one gate, and Rome has more than a dozen. Alaric also has a real grievance. The empire made him a general, he lost around 10,000 Goths fighting for a Roman emperor in 394, and Ravenna kept refusing his demands.

By late afternoon there are horns inside the walls, across the river on the west side. The sergeant either sits in the gate house and hands over the city, or leaves his only fortified ground and goes after the Goths. He goes. The cost is the walls. The squad covers two miles at a run and takes the near end of a stone bridge at sundown.

Then night flips the fight. Hundreds of Goths carry torches, and every torch lights its owner up. The Marines pull down their night vision goggles and see every face, every spear and an archer climbing onto a roof. The Goths can only see as far as their own firelight, so the men holding the fire are the blind ones. Torches drop one by one and hiss out in the river.

The Goths adapt. Archers get onto the riverside roofs, where the goggles have to look up to find them, and an arrow drops into the gap between a Marine's neck and the top of his plate. A javelin hits the sergeant dead center, and the plate stops the point and spreads the blow across his ribs. The mass charge then jams the narrow bridge with its own dead. Eight Marines and 800 rounds remain. We drop Rome from C to F because the squad can only hold one bridge, Rome has others, and the Goths are already inside the walls.

The burning Forum

D

The Goths take the Forum; the Marines fall back to the CapitolineUS Marines vs Alaric's Goths in the Forum and the Basilica Aemilia

Fire takes away the Marines' edge. Goths who crossed upstream put torches to the timber roofs. Night vision amplifies faint light, and a burning district is the opposite of faint, so the glare washes out the goggles. The sergeant flips them up and can see about 40 feet through the smoke. The hill is a few minutes' climb away, with high ground and stone underfoot. He takes the squad into the Forum instead, because that is where the screaming is.

The crowd is the problem. Hundreds of Romans pour through the lanes the rifles are covering, so every shot has to thread between civilians. The sergeant orders that nobody shoots a man who isn't holding a weapon, and the Marines can fire only when a gap opens. Goths slip through the crowd close enough to smell. Alaric named the churches of Peter and Paul as sanctuaries and the people who reached them were spared, so some of these Romans might have lived anyway.

The screaming at the Basilica Aemilia is real. Despite the name, it isn't a church. It's a marble market hall about as long as a football field, full of shops and bankers' counters, and families have barricaded themselves inside while a dozen Goths hack at the doors. The sergeant takes four Marines in. Inside, stone walls line the corridors between stalls and every shot lands like a slap to both ears. They fire at three feet, and he points and squeezes without aiming.

The gear starts failing. The sergeant's glasses fog in the heat, and a rifle jams with a sword already coming down. Tap the magazine, rack the charging handle, fire: two drilled seconds, and the blade misses the helmet by a thumb's width. Two seconds isn't always there. A spear catches a Marine under the arm, where the plate leaves him open. Another goes down under three Goths in a doorway, and a third is under the roof when it falls. Casualties: five.

The sergeant drops the Alamo plan. Armies like Alaric's rarely fight to the last man. They break, often when the men they follow go down, as the English did at Hastings in 1066 once King Harold was killed. He stops counting Goths and starts counting chiefs. With 350 rounds across five rifles, he needs ground where the chiefs must chase him one step at a time. That is the Capitoline, which the Gauls besieged for seven months in the story Romans told and never took. We lift Rome from F to D. The Forum is lost, but every Goth in the city is about to come to one stair.

The Hundred Steps of the Capitoline

B

The Marines; the Goths withdraw5 US Marines vs Alaric's Goths on the Hundred Steps

The stair does the work the walls couldn't. An army can't climb a stair all at once, and only the front few men can reach the top. Alaric's whole army becomes a short line, and five rifles can hold a short line. The Goths lock shields and come up behind a wall of wood, which is the right answer to arrows. Against rifles it's the gate all over again: the rounds go through the shields and into the men holding them, and the wall comes apart from the inside.

Each rush then climbs over the last one's dead. The fallen become the ground, so every Goth is a step higher and climbing into the rifles on a ramp built from his friends. The Marines reload in pairs and keep the fire steady, and the ammunition runs down: 200 rounds, then 150, then 90. Then a cluster of chiefs, gold at their wrists, shoves to the front. Their rush is the Goths' answer. The sergeant's order is all rifles on the chiefs, and it costs him his flanks.

He knows the risk. Tacitus wrote in Germania that a warrior who left a battle alive after his chief fell lived in lifelong shame. If that still holds, killing a chief feeds a charge, and for a few seconds it does. The first chief makes six steps and the second makes nine. The last chief falls three steps from the top, and the banner he carries drops onto the stone.

Then the other half of Tacitus's point arrives. He was describing a chief's own sworn companions. The thousands behind aren't sworn to anyone on that stair. Alaric's army is Goths, plus barbarian soldiers who left Roman service, plus slaves who joined him. They watch every chief die and stop, and then they back down one band at a time. One Marine dies to a spear an inch above his plate, so every Marine who died that night died where the plate wasn't. Casualties: six.

The sergeant's bolt locks back on an empty magazine, and nobody has a round left. The Goths walking down the hill don't know. We jump Rome from D to B, skipping a full grade. The city is standing and the Goths are leaving, but the Forum is ash and the squad has nothing left to shoot.

What it comes down to

Ten Marines can't kill 30,000 Goths with 2,100 rounds, and they don't try. At the gate they use range discipline and bullets that pass through shields. On the bridge they use night vision against torchlight. On the Hundred Steps they kill the chiefs where thousands of unsworn followers can see it, and the army quits. The plates save them every time except where they don't cover, and the Forum still burns. In the real sack the Basilica Aemilia burned hot enough to fuse bronze coins into its floor, and the Marines don't change that. They change who owns the morning.
