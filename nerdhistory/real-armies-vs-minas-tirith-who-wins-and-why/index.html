Real Armies vs Minas Tirith: Who Wins and Why

Four real armies get one assault each on Minas Tirith at full strength, and we score how far each one climbs.

Only the modern brigade reaches the Citadel; Minas Tirith beats the other three armies.

Every NerdHistory fight

The Assyrians Break Ground

F

Minas TirithAssyrians of Lachish vs Minas Tirith

The Assyrians open the way they took Lachish in 701 BC. Archers fire from behind shield-bearers. Heavy wheeled siege engines roll toward the Great Gate under wicker screens. Each has a wooden ram on chains tipped with a metal blade. Crews inside ladle water against fire arrows. Fire isn't what stops them.

The gate does. In Tolkien, the Great Gate is iron hung on posts of steel, so the ram rings off it like a bell. The crew moves to the wall, but the blade only has two jobs: pound and pry. Pounding does nothing here, and prying needs a seam to jam into, as it does between mud bricks. The black outer wall has none. It is faced like Orthanc, which Tolkien calls unconquerable by steel or fire.

So the commander reaches for what won Lachish: a ramp, where engineers piled roughly 20,000 tons of stone about 260 feet long. Sappers also try the old tunnel-and-burn trick, and Tolkien says the wall breaks only if the ground under it tears open. But every level is cut into the hill, so the picks hit rock. A ramp built one basket at a time sits inside bowshot, and it would only reach the first of seven walls.

The defenders make them pay for each basket. A boulder drops onto the lead engine, and fire arrows burn wicker faster than the ladles can put it out. In the first hour, about a third of the assault force is dead under the wall, and the gate hasn't moved. We score it an F. The Assyrian toolkit was built to pry apart mud brick, and this city answers with iron and a wall with no seams.

The Ottomans and the Seventh Ball

D

Minas TirithOttoman Empire (Orban's bombard) vs Minas Tirith

The Ottoman battery sets up about a mile out, built around the gun from the siege of Constantinople in 1453. Orban's bombard is a bronze barrel 27 feet long that throws a stone ball weighing over half a ton a mile or more. The catch is heat: every shot cooks the barrel, so a gun like this fires about seven times a day. Seven is the whole budget.

Shots one and two hit the outer wall beside the gate. The ball shatters and the wall doesn't. At Constantinople, the guns needed 53 days against ordinary stone and brick roughly 16 to 18 feet thick. This wall is black, Orthanc-grade stone. Even Sauron's own engineers in the book don't try to break it. Their catapults throw fire over it.

So the commander retargets the gate, which is iron and steel with no magic. In the book, the gate falls to Grond, a 100-foot ram with a black steel wolf's head and the Witch-king's spells behind every stroke. Raw force alone never faces it. Shot three goes wide. Archers can't answer a gunline a mile out, since a bow's reach is a few hundred yards at best. So cavalry ride out, cut the ropes on the timber beds and kill the crews who know how to aim. The gun is silent for hours.

Late in the day, shot four hits the gate square, and the doors bow inward a few inches with a dark seam between them. The Janissaries form up. Shots five and six burst on the stone bastion. At dusk the crew loads the seventh ball into a barrel that hasn't cooled. It cracks and bursts, tearing through the crew. Orban himself is said to have died at Constantinople when an overworked gun exploded.

Even an open gate wouldn't have saved them. A bombard on a timber bed is built to batter the wall in front of it, not lob shots over it. Past the gate, the road turns and climbs to six more walls. Each sits about 100 feet above the last, and each gate faces a different way. We score it a D. They dented the gate, never marked the wall, and the seventh ball killed their own gun.

Steel on the Switchbacks

C

Minas TirithSoviet Red Army (WWII) vs Minas Tirith

The Red Army opens with Katyusha rockets. A battery empties its rails in seconds and walks a line of fire up the outer wall. Archers on the parapet die or run for cover. The wall comes out of the smoke scorched and whole, because steel or fire won't break it. So the Soviets go where every army should go, the gate. A 152 mm gun-howitzer fires over open sights, a heavy shell about 6 inches across.

The first round hits the iron doors dead center and they hold. So does the second. But round after round on the same spot folds the doors off their posts. T-34s roll through with infantry riding on the hulls, the famous tank riders. No army before them got through the gate. It's the Soviets' best moment, and it lasts about 200 yards.

Past the gate, the road turns hard. A great keel of rock splits every level above the first, and the road punches through it in tunnels five times on the way up. Every gate faces a different way, so every climb is a switchback with a wall above it. Burning pitch splashes across the engine decks. One tank throws a track on the first hairpin and blocks the lane. Another stalls on the grade with its gearbox gone.

The column stops, so infantry go in on foot. Submachine gunners and sappers clear the first level house by house, and swordsmen counterattack in alleys too narrow for a rifle swing. A flamethrower turns one street into a river of fire. Casualties climb into the hundreds on both sides. But the hinge of the fight is back on the plain, where the howitzer crews lay their guns on a second wall. It's white stone, not Orthanc black, and the archers above are easy targets.

Their battery commander waves the crews off. Soviet infantry are now right under that wall, and artillery doctrine treats any strike close to friendly troops as danger close. Every target above the Soviets sits just over their own men's heads, and an unguided round of the era scatters over a wide patch. It's Stalingrad in reverse, where Chuikov hugged the Germans so their guns couldn't fire. Here the mountain does the hugging. We score it a C. They broke in, but the guns that reach every wall can't aim close enough to use.

The Brigade Hits the Dead Zone

A

Modern brigadeModern brigade (drones, guided rockets) vs Minas Tirith

At dusk, a drone feed locks onto archers above the Great Gate and a thermal box snaps around each one. A loitering munition, a drone that is also the bomb, drops onto the parapet. A laser-guided round follows into the same stretch of wall. A Javelin missile fired from well outside bowshot flies through a tower window. Then a strike aircraft drops a 500-pound guided bomb. The iron bars that shrugged off a ram are gone. A HIMARS battery far behind the lines waits for coordinates.

Night is what decides the first level. Thermal sights show every defender glowing white against cold stone, while the defenders can't see past their own torchlight. About 4,000 soldiers push into a garrison Tolkien never fully counts. The brigade clears the first level in about an hour. The obvious objection is that the mountain blocks the drones' radio links. It doesn't. The city sits on the eastern face of its hill, looking out over the Pelennor, so every level is in line of sight from the grass. Laser designators also love rough stone, and scattered reflection is what a laser-guided round homes in on.

Then the brigade hits the same problem the Soviets did. Nobody wants to drive a vehicle into a rock tunnel under a parapet, so the infantry climb on foot up a road that sits inside bowshot of every archer above. This is the dead zone, and it's the city's best moment. Arrows from two walls pin a squad at a tunnel mouth. A car-sized rock flattens the lead vehicle at the foot of the climb. In 1944, the attack dies right here.

But the brigade's guns barely scatter. A GPS-guided shell like the Army's Excalibur lands far closer to its aim point than an unguided round, so it can be used much nearer friendly troops. Farther up, HIMARS rockets come down from above onto the walkways behind the parapets. So the commander does what the Soviets never could. He calls fire onto the wall directly above his own soldiers. The fire walks up the mountain just ahead of the infantry, and every archer who stands to draw shows up as heat before he can loose.

Gate by gate, engineers blow the doors. Just before dawn, they reach the seventh gate, the door to the Citadel at the very top. It faces east, straight down at the Great Gate 700 feet below. They blow that too. We score it an A, with the Citadel held by sunrise. It's not an S, because the outer wall never cracked and the dead zone made them bleed.

What it comes down to

Every defense Tolkien gave Minas Tirith works inside bowshot: the seamless black wall, the iron gate, the rock tunnels and the switchbacks with no gates in line. Rams, stone balls and unguided shells either can't break it or can't be fired at it once troops are close. The modern brigade wins because precision lets it hit every level from outside bowshot, then land fire on the wall right above its own soldiers. Nobody has to knock a wall down.
