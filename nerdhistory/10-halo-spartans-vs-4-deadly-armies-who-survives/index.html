10 Halo Spartans vs 4 Deadly Armies: Who Survives?

Ten Halo Spartans in MJOLNIR armor, 60 rounds per rifle and no reinforcements face the Persian Immortals, Crusader knights, a Tiger tank and modern special forces.

Nine of ten Spartans survive, graded S, A, A and B.

Every NerdHistory fight

The Immortals

S

The Spartans10 Halo Spartans vs 10,000 Persian Immortals

These are Herodotus's Immortals, not the masked monsters from the movie 300. They are robed spearmen with wicker shields, backed by archers, in a corps that stayed at exactly 10,000 because every dead man was replaced at once. The Greek Spartans threw them back at Thermopylae in 480 BC. The replacement system is their real strength, but it only works if the enemy bleeds at the same rate they do.

The arrows come first, and most of them miss. Spartan reaction time is about 20 milliseconds, against about a quarter of a second for a normal person. Canon says the suit's gel layer increases reaction time by a factor of five, so the squad scatters before the volley lands. In tests, a heavy-bow arrow still carries roughly 75 to 90 joules after about 230 meters. MJOLNIR is an alloy shell over a titanium-based bodysuit and a gel layer that stiffens on impact, all wrapped in an energy shield. A bronze arrowhead against that is a thumbtack against a car door.

The Immortals stand in rows, so every rifle burst drops three or four of them. A dozen spearmen swarm one Spartan and hang off his arms. He goes to one knee and stands back up with three of them still on him. Halo canon says MJOLNIR's gel layer doubles the wearer's lifting capability. It also has Spartan Adriana-111 using a Mongoose ATV as a melee weapon against Grunts. Nothing the Immortals carry can hurt anyone, the back ranks see it, and one spearman drops his weapon and runs. The panic spreads and the formation breaks. The Spartans don't chase, which saves ammunition. We score it S because they brought a 3,000-year technology gap.

The Crusaders

A

The Spartans10 Halo Spartans vs Templars, Hospitallers and crossbowmen in Crusader Jerusalem

We set this in the Crusader kingdom decades after the 1099 siege, because the Templars only appeared about 20 years later and the Hospitallers were still running a hospital. The crossbows fire first. Tests on reconstructed heavy crossbows put a bolt at roughly 120 to 200 joules, well above a Persian arrow. The shields flicker at the edges but hold, and the bolts clang off the plating.

The horse is the one real danger. A 2022 study of medieval horse bones found many stood under 14.2 hands, about 4'10" at the shoulder, so they were pony-sized. Even so, a 1,300-pound horse at a gallop of about 25 mph carries roughly 37,000 joules, twice a .50-caliber round. A Spartan in armor weighs about 1,000 pounds. The lead Spartan takes a lance and a horse's chest at full speed, his shield drops to nothing, and he is thrown back several feet with the horse landing on him.

Six knights pile on with maces and axes. Flanged maces were cutting-edge weapons in the 1100s, but against this armor they ring like church bells. They don't need to break through. A shield only starts recharging after a few seconds without a hit, so constant swinging keeps it down. He stops waiting on it. He heaves the horse off, throws one knight into the next, and rips a rider from the saddle. He can't ride the horse. A horse comfortably carries about 20% of its body weight, around 200 pounds here, and he weighs half a ton.

The deciding move is the split. Five Spartans pin the knights from behind a ruined wall while five sprint wide at a full run (about 34 mph in our scaling), loop through the camp, and hit the rear of a formation still facing the wrong way. A Spartan's shield comes back after a few seconds out of the fight. A knight's cracked rib stays cracked. The Spartans reset and the Crusaders accumulate damage. One more lance knocks a Spartan flat, but the line collapses between the two groups. It is an A because it took more work than the Immortals. Nobody came close to dying.

Nazi Peak Army

A

The Spartans, at the cost of one Spartan10 Halo Spartans vs Waffen-SS, MG42 nests, Panzer IVs and Tigers in a 1944 French village

The numbers stop being comfortable here. The MG42 fires about 1,200 rounds a minute, 20 a second, and each round carries roughly 4,000 joules. The first squad hits the dirt behind a collapsed wall and the stone chews itself apart. A shield can't recharge while someone keeps shooting it, and these gunners know that. They stagger their bursts and swap barrels every 250 rounds, so the pressure never drops and the push stalls in the open.

Then a Tiger rolls out from behind a church. Its 22-pound shell leaves the 88 mm at over 2,500 feet per second, about 3 million joules. We scale the shield to the games, where a single sniper round strips it, not to the novel The Fall of Reach, where a test shows it taking far heavier fire. It doesn't change this fight. We treat a direct hit from a tank gun, like the Scorpion's 90 mm cannon in the games, as lethal to a Spartan. The rule is never get hit.

So the Spartans move. Four commandeer an Opel Blitz three-ton truck and race down a side street, and the Tiger's turret swings to follow it. On early models the Tiger's turret turns about 6 degrees a second, and its hydraulic traverse speeds up with engine revs. A Spartan running 34 mph at 50 yards crosses the gunner's sight at roughly 19 degrees a second, and the gunner has to catch him. The flankers get behind the tank, and rockets into the engine deck, thin steel over a gasoline engine, blow it up. Grenades dropped through open hatches finish the Panzer IVs.

The cellars are where the SS gets dangerous. A Panzerfaust punches through tank armor and is the one infantry weapon here that can kill a Spartan in one hit. But its range is short, only tens of yards, so the shooter has to let a Spartan get close, and against Spartan reflexes nobody gets to aim. Shotgun blasts clear the rooms.

This fight takes its first Spartan. Artillery walks down the street and two Fw 190 fighters strafe it. One Spartan, his shield already stripped by machine-gun fire, doesn't move fast enough. A shell lands beside him and the gel layer stiffens, but it has a limit. The last Tiger falls when the Spartans attack from three directions, since a turret can't point three ways at once. We score it A, with one of the ten gone.

Modern Special Units

B

The Spartans, barely9 Halo Spartans vs Delta Force, SAS, Navy SEALs and Spetsnaz in a night raid

These are the best humans on Earth, and they come in on a rehearsed night raid. Delta Force breaches the doors, the SAS drops in from the rooftops, SEALs take the stairwells, and Spetsnaz comes out of a side tunnel. Every operator has thermal optics. Spartans were originally built to put down human insurrections, so this is the enemy the program was made for. It is still the widest crack in the armor.

The sniper decides the fight. He fires a .50-caliber anti-materiel rifle from 200 yards and aims at the shield, not the man. A .50 round carries roughly 18,000 joules, about 100 times a crossbow bolt, and the Raufoss Mk 211 round has a tungsten penetrator. It is the same cartridge the Warthog's turret fires. In the games, the sniper rifle's 14.5 mm round, roughly 30,000 joules in real terms, strips a shield with one body shot. So we figure two .50 hits, maybe three, and after that it's only the alloy.

The operators also have the sensor edge. Spartan eyes are augmented for near darkness, not heat. The motion tracker has a limited range and misses anything moving slowly or standing still in the early games. These operators are trained to do exactly that. Smoke and flashbangs blind the Spartans while the operators see straight through on thermal. Four-man teams overlap their fire, so a Spartan in a doorway has three barrels on him. A Javelin takes out half a stairwell and a drone feeds positions to everyone.

Then the sniper pins two Spartans in a courtyard. The first shield collapses in a spray of blue sparks, the second Spartan drags him behind a planter, and the next round chews the planter in half. Both shields are gone, and one more clean hit would kill someone. For about a minute, the operators are winning.

The squad pulls back inside, out of the sniper's sightline and away from the Javelin. The pinned pair wait for the one gap every sniper has, the magazine change, and cover 40 feet to the door in under a second. Inside, drywall doesn't stop a half-ton man, and Spartan reflexes beat a trigger finger. A carbine round carries about a tenth of the .50's punch. The kill zone flips and the hunters run. We score it B. Nobody else dies, but it sits below Normandy because they were losing for a full minute.

What it comes down to

Nine of the ten Spartans walk out of 25 centuries of enemies. Armor didn't carry them. Speed, fast reflexes and staying out of the sights of the few weapons that could kill them did. Bronze, wicker and lances couldn't touch them, and a shield that recharges outlasts a knight who doesn't heal. The Tiger's 88 and the SS found the first crack. The sniper-led special forces found the widest, and that is why the grades fall from S to A, A and B.
