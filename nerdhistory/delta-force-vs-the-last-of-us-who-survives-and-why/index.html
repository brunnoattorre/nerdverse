Delta Force vs The Last of Us: Who Survives and Why

Six real Delta Force operators hit Outbreak Day in Boston with blanks in their rifles, then face FEDRA, Clickers, a Bloater and a cannibal compound.

Delta Force lasts under a week outside the wall; one man walks out.

Every NerdHistory fight

Outbreak Day

A-

Five or six operators survive the first night; one corporal is lostSix Delta Force operators vs Runners

Runners are freshly infected people, and they still have eyes, lungs and a normal body under the fungus. That means a rifle round that drops a person drops them, and the squad's training works. Six shooters pick a target, drop it and shift. It holds for about 40 seconds.

Then the numbers take over. Take a full sprint of about 15 mph, or 22 feet every second, and a corner 50 yards away is roughly 7 seconds off. The infected pile into one lane faster than six rifles can clear it, and the lead truck stalls against the bodies. Each operator carries the Army's basic load of 210 rounds, seven 30-round magazines, and nobody is printing more.

The bitten neighbor shows the other rule. In canon, a bite is a countdown of hours to two days before the host turns, so the medic keeps every wounded person at arm's length from then on. The spores don't threaten them yet either. Spores take time to build up, and real Cordyceps pushes its fruiting body out of a dead ant only several days after death. On day one, teeth are the only way in.

We score it an A minus. Fire discipline and clean movement keep five or six people breathing through the first night. But the youngest rifleman, a 26-year-old corporal, gets cut off by a second pack at the stock room, and no playbook covers an enemy that ignores suppression.

The Quarantine Zone and FEDRA

B

Delta Force, at a cost: a rifleman and a FEDRA minder dieDelta Force vs FEDRA and raiders

The first fight is a negotiation. Soldiers take every rifle, sidearm and pair of night vision goggles at the gate, and nobody in that line has ever seen the promised receipt. Mercer saves the sniper's rifle by selling the unit as the only one in the city that has seen what's coming. The officer agrees. In The Last of Us, a uniform can kill you as fast as the fungus, as the franchise's opening shows when a soldier shoots Joel's daughter. That favor costs 20 years.

By 2033, the year the first game's story begins, FEDRA runs Boston like a prison. The squad spends two decades as the officer's off-book muscle. They're older, not soft: Joel is around 50 in the game and still takes on a Bloater. Tonight's job is a cache of medicine and ammo, and raiders ambush it a mile past the wall.

Ammo and armor decide the firefight. The breacher burns a 30-round magazine in 4 seconds, which is restrained, since a carbine on full auto cycles 700 to 900 rounds a minute. Two spare magazines later he's on his sidearm. Then the rifleman drags the wounded FEDRA soldier, Dolan, back by his vest. A standard medium plate is about 9.5 by 12.5 inches. It covers the heart and lungs but not the collar, and the second shot lands just above it.

The sniper answers by reading the muzzle flash and putting one round through the window behind it, which breaks the raiders. But gunfire is a dinner bell, and the infected pour in. The squad escapes through a storm drain and over a collapsed overpass, and the officer's cache is left rotting in the muck. We score it a B: 20 years of surviving a system built to squeeze them, undone in a night.

The Outside: Clickers and a Bloater

C

Delta Force escapes, losing the medicDelta Force vs Clickers and a Bloater

The Clicker is the poster monster of The Last of Us, and it's the one enemy Delta was built for. In canon it takes about a year of infection to form. By then the fungus has destroyed the eyes, so it hunts by echolocation, clicking and listening, with a short range. Moving without sound is literally the job.

So the first Clicker dies the way the game teaches. When the breacher's magazine hits the tile, Mercer taps his ear, tests the ground and lobs a glass bottle into the far corner. The Clicker spins and charges the noise, and the sniper drives a combat knife up through the base of the skull. A blade from behind ends it without a sound.

The second one is about the gear they gave up. Their night vision went into the officer's crate 20 years ago, so in the dark stairwell nobody sees it coming after the medic's boot catches a glass shard. It drags him off. Mercer forces the breacher's rifle down, because a single shot would call every Clicker in the building.

The basement is the Bloater. After years of infection, its fungal plating soaks up fire: the breacher's 5.56 mm rounds don't stop it. The gas masks handle the spores, and the infected share one documented weakness, fire. Mercer's kerosene bottles burn around 1,800°F and the Bloater goes up. We score it a C. They cross a Clicker nest nearly silent, but four walk in and three walk out, and no one can patch a wound anymore.

Hunters, Cannibals and the Final Stand

D

The compound; Mercer is the only operator left aliveThree starving operators vs a cannibal compound and a chained Bloater

The compound is a larder: roasting meat, shapes in butcher paper on hooks, one still with a boot. In canon, Hunters and cannibals are separate groups. Pittsburgh Hunters kill travelers for gear in the first game, and David's people eat them in the Colorado winter. This place does both. Chaining up infected also comes from canon: in Part II, the Rattlers do exactly that and use them as guard dogs.

The breach works at first. The sniper drops the tower guard from a barn roof as the gate charge blows, and Mercer and the breacher clear the first rooms in 6 seconds. Four men are down before any of them reach a weapon. It's the old days again.

Then sleep decides it. The CDC says 24 hours awake impairs you about like a 0.10% blood alcohol level, over the 0.08% legal limit for driving. The breacher has been up 48 hours. At the second doorway his eyes slide off the corner he should check, he steps through without clearing it, and a chained Bloater tears through the plaster and sends him into a shelf of cans.

Ammo finishes the job. In the concrete stairwell the magazines are nearly dry and the Bloater is splintering the door below. A frag grenade can't collapse a building, since it's 6.5 ounces of explosive with a kill radius of about 16 feet, but in a concrete shaft 16 feet is everything inside it. The sniper pulls the pin and shoves Mercer upward. The breacher, hurt by the Bloater's swing, dies of internal bleeding on the roof. We score it a D, not an F, because someone walks out. But two of three die and the compound keeps everything.

What it comes down to

The squad scores A-, B, C and D, which averages out to C++. Discipline kept them alive for 20 years behind a wall, and it handled the Clicker, the one enemy Delta was trained for. Outside, it lasted less than a week. Discipline can't refill a magazine or give back a night's sleep, so the infected were never the real killer. Ammo, sleep and teammates ran out first. Delta Force can survive The Last of Us for a while, just not as a team.
