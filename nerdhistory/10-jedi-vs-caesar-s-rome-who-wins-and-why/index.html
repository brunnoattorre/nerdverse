10 Jedi vs Caesar's Rome: Who Wins and Why

Ten Jedi with only lightsabers and the Force try to conquer Rome at the height of Julius Caesar's power, graded chapter by chapter from F to S.

The Jedi win every fight but lose the war; Rome never falls.

Every NerdHistory fight

Outskirts Infiltration

B

The Jedi, at the cost of Dren and their coverTen Jedi vs the Appian Way checkpoint and a 30-rider cavalry patrol

The Jedi start with a mind trick, and it leans on the rule Obi-Wan states in A New Hope: the Force has a strong influence on the weak-minded. Master Voss works it on the centurion and four tired soldiers on a boring shift, and they wave the group through. The sixth soldier wears three charms and feels something crawl across his mind. He panics, screams that the gods are in his head, and grabs the Padawan Dren by the sleeve.

That ruins the stealth. Dren's saber ignites, a trumpet sounds, and 30 riders come down the hill throwing javelins. The Jedi circle the carts with their blades spinning, and the javelins come apart in midair. Cesra, a young knight, pushes three riders out of their saddles in one move. Within two minutes the patrol has no javelins left and the road is clear.

Roman steel can't answer a lightsaber. Consider The Phantom Menace, where Qui-Gon pushes his blade into a blast door and the metal melts around it. Roman blades are iron, so there is no clash: a rider's sword just ends a few inches above his hand. The riders wear chainmail, heavy iron rings, and a saber parts it the same way. The segmented plate from the movies isn't what these riders are wearing.

The B tier is about what the win cost. Every fight is won and the stealth is gone. The javelin Dren never sees, thrown while he guards a merchant's cart against two riders, kills him. Nine Jedi remain. Voss also picks up a fresh silver coin with Caesar's living face on it, something Rome's mint had never struck before.

The Legion Gauntlet

C

The Jedi, barely, after three hoursNine Jedi vs a full Roman legion with its scorpions

A legion on paper is several thousand men, and this one has dozens of scorpions behind it, twisted-rope crossbows that fire iron-tipped bolts. Going around would only mean meeting it again at the walls, so Voss goes through. Four Jedi defend the front, four work the flanks as the hammer, and Voss calls targets from the middle. The first volley lands before the infantry moves, and the defenders knock every bolt into the dirt.

The testudo is what the Jedi break first. The tortoise only works because every soldier is packed shoulder to shoulder and leaning on the man beside him. That is also its flaw. Cesra drives one Force push into the front corner and the box folds instead of denting: first rank into the second, second into the third. A formation built to share weight shares the fall. Ranks get flung into the olive trees ten and twenty men at a time.

The number that decides the chapter is the scorpion count: we put it at 60 machines, about one per century, each firing three or four shots a minute. Assume only one bolt in ten is truly on target. Over three hours that is still about 1,000 blocks for every defender, with no misses allowed. Roman lines also rotate, so tired men step back and fresh men step forward. The Jedi never get a rest.

Jedi don't react faster. They react earlier. In The Phantom Menace Qui-Gon says Anakin can see things before they happen, which is why he appears to have such quick reflexes. But that edge runs on focus, and canon shows focus running out: in The Mandalorian, lifting a mudhorn makes Grogu pass out from Force exhaustion. By hour three Tavo sees the heavy bolt and knows where it will be, but his saber arm shakes and the blade arrives late. He dies. The Jedi spend everything on one last push, the legion's ranks fold, and the survivors scatter. That is the C tier: they won the ground but lost a friend and most of their strength.

The Gates and Streets of Rome

D

The Jedi hold the Forum, but lose two moreEight Jedi vs the Porta Capena, Caesar's veterans, Gaius Brutus and the burning oil of the Forum

Caesar's Rome has the old Servian wall, about 7 miles around and up to 33 feet high. The 12-mile brick walls people picture are the Aurelian walls, built in AD 271 to 275, more than three centuries away. At the Porta Capena the gate is barred from inside, so Cesra jumps. In our reading of the prequels, a typical Force jump is around 15 feet, so she does it in two, kicking off the stone for the second boost. Voss and two others follow and crack the wooden bar with a Force push.

Inside, the streets are narrow, with apartment blocks several stories high, and the defenders are Caesar's veterans, not Praetorians. The permanent Praetorian Guard doesn't exist yet, since Augustus creates it in 27 BC, about 17 years later. In the Subura, archers shoot straight down on the crowd, and every deflected arrow has to land somewhere. Oran bats one arrow back at the archer's perch, then turns his blade to catch the next as it drops toward fleeing women. A veteran's spear goes in between his ribs. Voss then orders the civilians shielded first, even at a cost in time and strength.

At the Forum, Gaius Brutus, a gladiator with 11 campaigns behind him, throws an overhead cut. Voss sidesteps and the saber shears the sword off a hand's width above the hilt, the cut edge glowing orange. Brutus is left with a stub, and Voss puts him down with a gentle push and takes his helmet instead of his life. This is the lightsaber doing what it does best against iron.

Then the rooftops use something a lightsaber can't parry. The saber stops anything that arrives in one piece, but oil doesn't. Hot olive oil is dangerous long before it boils, and a jar from a 50-foot roofline hits the ground in under two seconds, so the only defense is the Force. Voss clears three jars. There are 30. Kelnacca throws herself over a huddle of children and holds a Force barrier, as Grogu blocks and redirects the flames of a flamethrower in The Mandalorian. The oil slides off, but the heavy pitch behind it cracks the barrier. She dies, and the Forum falls to the Jedi at a D-tier cost, with six Jedi left.

Showdown with Caesar

D

Caesar, and Rome, even as the Jedi capture himSix Jedi vs Julius Caesar and a hundred veterans in the Temple of Concord

Caesar isn't trying to win the fight. He's trying to outlast it. A hundred veterans who held the line at Alesia lock a shield ring around the far end of the hall. He sends them at the Jedi in waves of ten, pulls them back and sends ten fresh ones. Every wave forces the Jedi to push, deflect and move, and none of it ends anything. On the fourth wave a knight swings late and a gladius finds him. The fifth wave kills the knight beside him.

Voss then stops defending. He and the three knights left drive a tight wedge into one corner of the ring, the same move Cesra used on the testudo. Packed men share weight and share the fall, so the ring opens like a door. Nothing is left between the Jedi and Caesar's chair. Voss uses the Force to order the legions to stand down, and Caesar's eyes go distant for one heartbeat.

The trick fails because of the rule from chapter one: the Force influences the weak-minded. Caesar isn't weak-minded. As a young man, pirates held him for ransom, and the sum came to 50 talents. He later returned with a fleet and had them executed, per Plutarch and Suetonius. That is not a man whose will bends easily. The trick lands just enough to make his guards lower their swords, and no further.

Even a Caesar who obeyed wouldn't hand over Rome. Roman custom treated a citizen in enemy hands as having lost his standing until he came home. The best-known example is the tale of Regulus, said to have been captured by Carthage and sent to Rome to argue for peace, who told the Senate to refuse. Caesar knows the story, and so does every officer outside. Mark Antony gives the orders, and the legions mustering for the Parthian war don't need Caesar in the room. A captured Caesar has no authority, so the legions march on, and the Jedi are left with Caesar in hand and nothing to trade him for.

What it comes down to

The Jedi win every fight: a lightsaber beats Roman iron, and the Force breaks the testudo and the shield wall. They lose the war because the Force works on minds, and Rome isn't a mind. It is a 700-year-old system with a replacement ready for any one man, even Caesar, who will be stabbed to death by senators within weeks while Rome keeps going anyway. Four of ten Jedi walk out, and we grade the whole run D.
