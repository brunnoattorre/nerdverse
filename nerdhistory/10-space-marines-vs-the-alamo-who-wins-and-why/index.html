10 Space Marines vs the Alamo: Who Wins and Why

Ten Ultramarines drop into the Alamo at 3 a.m. on March 6, 1836, and we grade four chapters of their stand from F to S.

The Alamo still falls, but five Marines walk out alive: tier A.

Every NerdHistory fight

The Night Before

A

The squad takes command and builds a real defense, but the ammo math is already losingUltramarines Tactical Squad vs the Alamo's odds (about 1,800 Mexican soldiers outside)

The squad is one Ultramarines Tactical Squad under an unnamed Sergeant Titus. It is not Demetrian Titus from the Space Marine games, an officer who outranks any squad sergeant. It has seven bolters, one heavy bolter and one plasma gun, all in Mk VII power armor. It lands at 3 a.m. in a drop pod, and nobody has been hurt yet.

Crockett doubts them until Brother Marcus fires a bolter at a stack of logs 100 yards away. A bolter fires self-propelled explosive shells. A charge pushes each one out of the barrel, then a tiny rocket motor takes over, and the warhead detonates just after it punches in. The top log breaks from the inside out. That is why Crockett asks where Titus wants him.

Titus uses the hour well. The belt-fed heavy bolter goes on the upper floor of the long barracks, covering the plaza and north wall. The plasma gunner takes the north wall's weak spot. Two Marines watch the east and south walls on infrared, and Decius takes the chapel doors. The garrison's 21 muzzle-loading cannons impress him little, since each needs a crew of about six and is slow to reload.

The chapter ends on the problem that decides the later fights. A bolter magazine holds about 30 rounds, and 1,800 men is 60 full magazines. That assumes every round drops a man, which will not happen in a dark, moving crowd. Titus sees the problem and sets his positions anyway.

The First Wave Breaks

B

The Marines break the first wave, but the Alamo gets smallerUltramarines vs Santa Anna's assault columns

Santa Anna's columns come at the north wall around 5:30, packed shoulder to shoulder with ladders. Titus holds fire until they are in range. Then the heavy bolter opens up, and every round bursts inside the front ranks, which have nowhere to spread out. Frag grenades follow, and the column folds in on itself. In under two minutes the assault is bodies and broken ladders.

The discipline matters as much as the guns. The Marines fire short controlled bursts and call their remaining rounds over the Vox, so Titus always knows the count. That is the Ultramarines way, from the Codex Astartes written by their founder. Crockett's riflemen shoot the officers, and each fallen coat makes a section of the line hesitate.

The Mexican army's Brown Bess ball weighs roughly an ounce and leaves the barrel at about 1,000 feet per second. That is roughly 1,300 to 1,700 joules, around three times a 9mm pistol round. Power armor is built to shrug off far heavier fire than that, so the soft lead flattens against the ceramite and leaves a scuff. Muskets only matter in hundreds, and they come one at a time.

The cost comes from elsewhere. Santa Anna's light guns chew the top of the north wall, the part the Marines shoot over, until they stand in the open. The plasma gun overheats and locks up. Marcus charges with a combat knife, and a bayonet finds the joint under his arm. His Larraman cells seal the wound in seconds. The squad is down to 40% ammunition, with one Marine wounded, so Titus pulls back. That is why the tier is B.

The Wall Falls

C

The Mexican army takes the north wall, then the barracks and chapelUltramarines vs the combined Mexican assault and the captured Texian guns

The next assault comes from the north, east and south at once. Engineers put ladders on the ruined north wall, the heavy bolter runs down to its last belt, and the wall is lost. The Marines kill dozens in close fighting, but two soldiers climb in for every one who drops. Then the bolters click empty one by one. This is the 3 a.m. math coming due, and Titus charges a company of grenadiers with his chain sword.

The mission still fails to the Texian cannons, not the muskets. The Mexican army captures the 18-pounder at the southwest corner and turns the garrison's own guns on the long barracks and chapel. That happened in the real 1836 battle: a shot from the captured 18-pounder destroyed the barricade at the chapel door. An 18-pound iron ball carries hundreds of thousands of joules, hundreds of musket balls' worth in one hit. Lead bounces off ceramite, but a wrecking ball at point-blank range does not.

Decius shows the difference at the chapel doors. For 20 minutes bayonets find his joints, his Larraman cells seal the wounds, and musket balls spark off his chest. The infantry cannot move him, so they swing the 18-pounder around to face the doorway, as in the real battle. Decius takes the shot square in the chest and slides down the door frame. The long barracks goes the same way, room by room.

The garrison breaks on schedule too. Bowie fires from his cot with two pistols and his knife while Calix holds the doorway, which matches accounts of the real Bowie fighting from his cot with pistols and knife. Travis falls on the north wall. The Texians run for the buildings. Four Marines are down, six remain with no ammo, no walls and no help, so the tier is C.

The Last Stand

A

The Alamo falls, but five Marines escape and the Mexican army loses over 1,000Ultramarines vs Santa Anna's reserve at the chapel and palisade

Six Marines fall back into the chapel. Titus sees that holding there means the captured guns kill them one doorway at a time. So the squad forms a wedge and drives into the courtyard toward Santa Anna. Musket hits strip the blue paint to bare gray ceramite, but the wedge walks through the soldiers. The conscripts are cold, hungry and have watched giants cut through their friends for an hour. The formation comes apart, and Santa Anna steps backward.

Then Titus sees Crockett at the palisade, out of powder and swinging his rifle like a club. He turns back, and the Marine and the former congressman fight back to back. The main gate gives way and the reserve floods in. Crockett goes down. His cap is left in the dirt beside him. Survivor Susanna Dickinson later said she recognized Crockett's body and remembered seeing his peculiar cap by his side. How he died is still disputed.

Marcus, stiff from the bayonet wound, takes the powder magazine Titus found at 3 a.m. and tells the others to go. In the real battle, the Irish master of ordnance Robert Evans was shot down carrying a torch toward that powder. Here Marcus finishes Evans's job with his last grenade, and the blast buries the entrance in burning timber.

The tier is A because the squad lives even though the mission does not. Five Marines vanish on foot through the smoke. The Mexican army counts over a thousand dead and wounded, nearly double the roughly 600 the real battle is estimated to have cost. Ten Marines still cannot hold a quarter mile of wall alone, and the Texians they came to protect are gone.

What it comes down to

The muskets never decided anything. Titus's math did: a magazine of about 30 rounds against 1,800 men, then the garrison's own cannons turned against the Marines. Santa Anna's infantry could not get through the armor, and the 18-pounder could. Ten Marines cost the Mexican army nearly double its real losses and still could not hold a quarter mile of crumbling adobe. Five walked out, so the squad earns an A while the Alamo falls.
