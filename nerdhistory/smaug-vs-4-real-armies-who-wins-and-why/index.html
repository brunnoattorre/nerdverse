Smaug vs 4 Real Armies: Who Wins and Why

Four real armies get one night at Lake-town to stop Smaug, scored F to S by the rules of Tolkien's book, not the movie.

Only the Patriot battery stops Smaug; the other three armies fall.

Every NerdHistory fight

Byzantine Greek Fire

D

SmaugByzantine Greek fire crews vs Smaug

Greek fire is a ship killer. The best guess at the recipe is crude oil from Black Sea seeps, thickened with pine resin. It sticks, and it burns on water. Byzantine fleets used it against galleys made of wood, pitch and canvas. Smaug is none of those. When the 2002 rebuild was tested, it reached only about 50 ft, so the first tower's stream falls short as Smaug passes at speed.

The second tower learns. They lead him and fire when he banks inside 50 ft, and the burning oil does cling to his flank. It doesn't matter. That 2002 rebuild measured flames over 1,830°F. Smaug says in the book that he wears iron scales, and iron doesn't melt until about 2,800°F. The fire burns out and leaves a black smear.

Smaug also doesn't need to put the fire out. Tolkien is clear that he stays away from the deep, cold lake, and that the water would kill him. Greek fire shrugs off water anyway, so he flies on with flames streaming off him.

Then he turns on the tower. His breath hits it dead center, and the hand-pumped fuel reservoirs rupture. The clay grenades crack on their racks and a chain of fireballs runs down the dock. In the book, Lake-town soaks its roofs and his fire lights them anyway. The weapon that broke Arab fleets at Constantinople in 678 and 718 ends up burning its own crew. We score it a D.

English Longbowmen

D

SmaugEnglish longbowmen vs Smaug

These archers are strong. War bows raised from Henry VIII's warship, the Mary Rose, drew 100 to 185 lbs, and the archers' skeletons came up lopsided from a lifetime of pulling them. A good archer looses 10 or 12 shafts a minute, each with roughly 100 to 150 joules behind it. A narrow bodkin can punch through mail and, in some tests at very close range, even plate. Smaug's armor is iron scales over hard gems, and the first volleys skip off or miss.

Tolkien already ran this test. Lake-town's archers shot at Smaug in the book, and their arrows bounced off his scales and gems. Bard, the man who finally killed him, shot a great yew bow, and the English longbow is a yew bow too. The longbowmen hold Bard's weapon. What they lack is his information.

The captain times a single massed volley at the spot where Smaug will be, and hundreds of shafts hit him at once. Smaug doesn't bank, because in the book he flies straight through the arrows, too furious to turn his armored sides toward the archers. The shafts fall away broken. He climbs, the men think he is retreating, and he comes back down belly-first along the rooftops.

That is the moment from the book. His belly is crusted with gems except for one bare patch on the left breast, and it faces a few hundred bows. Bard had a thrush on his shoulder telling him where to aim. Nobody on these roofs does, so the captain calls the volley at open sky and not one shaft finds the patch.

Smaug's breath then sweeps the waterfront. In the book, the roofs were soaked with water and still lit. This thatch is bone dry, so the whole line goes up faster than the men can climb down, with the bows, quivers and unfired arrows burning with them. Same grade as the Byzantines, because Lake-town's archers got the same result in Tolkien. We score it a D.

WWII Flak 88 Battery

B

SmaugGerman 88 mm Flak 36 battery vs Smaug

This is the first army that doesn't need a direct hit. Each 20 lb shell carries a bursting charge and throws jagged steel in every direction, and one fingernail-sized fragment carries several times the energy of a war arrow. Tolkien never tests Smaug against anything heavier than an arrow, so nobody can say for certain that a fragment goes through iron scales. But it is more than anything that has touched him tonight.

The battery is four Flak 36 guns, each firing 15 to 20 rounds a minute, with a central director. The director is a mechanical computer with a 13 ft rangefinder that sends firing solutions to dials on each gun. For a few seconds the math is right. Then Smaug breaks the one assumption it was built on. This early director expects a target on a straight course, and the next model was redesigned to handle turning targets. Bombers fly straight. Smaug wheels, drops and climbs.

A shell needs about 2 seconds to reach a target a mile out, and in 2 seconds he is somewhere else. Then luck does what the math couldn't. A burst goes off close under his belly, and the shock wave and hot steel slam into the gem crust and knock him sideways. For the first time all night something has moved him. That burst went off a couple of yards from the bare patch on his left breast. In the book one arrow there killed him, and a fragment with several times an arrow's energy would almost certainly have done the same. The battery was one lucky splinter from ending the fight.

They don't get it. Every shot leaves an orange muzzle flash pointing back at the guns, and Tolkien's Smaug is a thinker. From a single riddling boast, "barrel-rider", plus the ponies and camps outside, he works out that Lake-town helped the dwarves. Reading a flash is easier. He drops low over the water and comes in across the battery's front, where five tons of gun on hand wheels can't swing fast enough. One long breath sweeps the line. Propellant starts cooking off at a few hundred degrees Fahrenheit, and his fire lights wet thatch, so the ready racks go first and the whole position erupts. We score it a B for moving him and nearly killing him.

Modern Patriot Battery

S

The Patriot batteryPatriot PAC-3 missile battery vs Smaug

This Patriot battery fields four truck-mounted launchers with 16 PAC-3 interceptors each. Each missile weighs about 700 lb and reaches roughly Mach 5, around 3,800 mph. Its flat radar steers its beam electronically, and the interceptor carries its own radar in the nose. It has almost no warhead, only a small charge that throws a ring of metal pellets. The kill comes from the collision. The book also hands the battery a gift. Smaug brags about his iron scales, and to a radar iron isn't armor, it is a mirror.

Smaug does something no other target did tonight. When the first interceptor comes in, he breaks hard at the last instant and turns inside it. The missile fires 180 small side thrusters meant to nudge it into a body-to-body hit and still overshoots by a few yards. The battery fires in pairs, though, and the dodge sets up the second missile. He spent his turn and is now committed to a dive down the smoke trail with nothing left to break with.

Here is the number that decides the whole night. Say the second interceptor has burned off half its weight and slowed to Mach 3 by the time it arrives. That is still more than 50 million joules, at least the energy of 25 lb of TNT. A war arrow like the one that killed Smaug in the book carries maybe 150 joules, so the interceptor hits more than 300,000 times harder. It strikes him square in the chest, a hand's width from the bare patch.

In Tolkien, finding that patch took a thrush, an heirloom black arrow and an archer who could understand birds. The Patriot doesn't need to find it. Smaug shoots upward on instinct, exactly the way Tolkien wrote his death, then turns over and falls. He lands in the lake, the one thing in town stronger than him, and the water quenches him in a column of steam. We score it an S, because it is the only weapon that hits so hard the patch stops mattering.

What it comes down to

Tolkien's rules made the question whether anything gets through at all. Greek fire and longbows don't, and both get a D. The Flak 88s came within one lucky splinter of the bare patch and score a B. The Patriot is the only army that survives, because its interceptor hits hundreds of thousands of times harder than the arrow that killed Smaug in the book and doesn't need Bard's thrush. The longbowmen held Bard's weapon at Bard's moment, one bird short.
