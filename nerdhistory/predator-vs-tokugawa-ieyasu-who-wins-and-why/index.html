Predator vs Tokugawa Ieyasu: Who Wins and Why

A Yautja hunter stalks Tokugawa Ieyasu days after Mikatagahara, and we grade each of four hunts by who controls the ground.

Ieyasu survives; the Predator fails every hunt on ground he chose.

Every NerdHistory fight

The Siege of Hamamatsu

A

Tokugawa Ieyasu (escapes)The Predator vs Tokugawa Ieyasu

On the night the Takeda army beat him at Mikatagahara in January 1573, Ieyasu got home and left Hamamatsu's gates open with every brazier lit, so his stragglers could find the way. The Takeda vanguard smelled a trap and held off. That story is why the castle is still burning bright when the Predator lands days later. The hunter kills two guards on the wall cloaked, but Ieyasu spots torch smoke bending around something that isn't there.

The Predator's best moment comes next. It rushes Ieyasu, and he turns the twin wrist blades on his katana, but the gauntlet tears his sleeve open. He runs up a stair barely wide enough for one armored man. The plasma caster's targeting laser slides off the timber, so the hunter blasts the gate beam beside his head. The beam detonates, the shock wave throws him onto his left shoulder, and his lacquered iron armor holds. A near miss, and the stair is the reason it stays one.

Fire decides it. Thermal vision finds warm bodies against cool backgrounds, which is why Dutch hid under mud in the first Predator. Hamamatsu is the opposite of mud: a torch flame burns far hotter than a man's body at about 98.6°F, so Ieyasu is a candle in a bonfire. The mask can switch modes, as it did in Predator 2 to catch a heat-shielded strike team by their ultraviolet lights, but that costs a full second.

A second is all Ieyasu needs. He kicks a brazier off the landing onto the supply carts below, and hay, oil jars and dry timber go up in a column of flame. The sensor goes white, and by the time the hunter finds a working mode he has limped off into the smoke. The Predator didn't lose a fight, it lost its target, which is why we give it an A.

The Kill Zone

B

Tokugawa Ieyasu (escapes)The Predator vs Tokugawa Ieyasu and his arquebusiers

The Predator lays a dead sentry in the middle of the courtyard with his spear snapped, and Ieyasu treats it as a challenge. His men flood the packed earth into mud, because anything that lands in it leaves a splash and you can't cloak a splash. Dozens of arquebusiers climb into the rafters of the long hall with slow match glowing. Ieyasu then stands alone in the middle, helmet under his arm and katana sheathed, as the bait.

The bait works because of how the species behaves. In the first Predator, the hunter takes off its own mask and weapons to fight Dutch hand to hand. This one sees one general with a sword and decides the guns don't count. It leaps down into the water with a heavy splash, and the volley starts.

The guns alone don't do it. Each arquebus throws a heavy lead ball, roughly half an inch across on a typical 3-monme gun, and people underrate them. But alien plate isn't period steel, and most of the volley sparks off or misses the shimmer. The water does the real work. The first two films both show water shorting out the cloak, and here it flickers like a bad torch and dies, leaving a towering alien in the open.

The hunter hits back hard. A net gun pins two gunners to a post, and the smart disc from Predator 2 slices the crossbeams so timber and men crash down together. But the throw costs half a step and the mud costs another. One gunner still clinging to a beam fires, and the ball hits the high left shoulder where the plate ends. Green blood proves what the first film said: it bleeds. Ieyasu slips out through a hidden wall panel and bars it. Tier B: the Predator is hurt, not finished.

The Shrine Duel

D

Tokugawa IeyasuThe Predator vs Tokugawa Ieyasu

Ieyasu knows a wounded Predator won't walk into another gun line, so he gives it what it wants: him, alone. He climbs to a hilltop shrine with one lantern, while Honda Tadakatsu, one of his leading generals, waits at the foot of the steps with a few spearmen, ordered to stay out. The Predator follows past midnight with its shoulder patched from its field kit, as the first film's hunter treated the wound the commandos' gunfire gave it. Patched isn't healed, and the shrine's narrow halls and low beams scrape its bulky armor. The cloak only sputters.

Ieyasu watches the paper screens, not the door. Lanterns outside make every screen glow from behind, and anything crossing the glow drags a ripple of warped light, the smoke trick on paper. When the third screen bursts, the twin blades hit where his chest just was. His katana snaps across the forearm and the cloak spits static. He steps off the line instead of meeting it, which we credit to his training in the Shinkage sword lineage, the school the Tokugawa shogunate later made one of its official styles.

The fight turns on a gamble. His left shoulder is stiff from the fall on the stair, and the Predator reads it, slams into him and pins him to a pillar by the throat with his sword arm jammed wide. The blades find the seam on the right side of his breastplate where samurai armor ties shut, and open his ribs. Ieyasu goes slack and lets the katana droop. To a hunter that has seen it in 100 hunts, that is a body giving up.

It leans in to finish him, and that puts the seam of its mask, where face plate meets housing, right in front of him. He drives the katana up with both hands, the point goes into the seam, and the mask cracks. The franchise has precedent: in Predators (2010), Hanzo, armed with a katana, and the Falconer kill each other. The left lens goes dark and green fluid sprays. A split mask, one working eye and a patched shoulder is why the tier drops to D.

The Last Move

F

Ieyasu and Honda Tadakatsu survive; the Predator is destroyedThe Predator vs Tokugawa Ieyasu and Honda Tadakatsu

The Predator drags its claw across the gauntlet keypad and the self-destruct starts. It crosses the room and clamps both hands on the front of Ieyasu's breastplate, because a bomb only works if the target stays inside it. Ieyasu hacks at the gauntlet, since in the lore wrecking the device mid-countdown can stop it, but his blade skids off the alloy. Honda Tadakatsu breaks the order to stay out and comes through the broken screens with his men.

Ieyasu stops fighting the grip and saws through his own shoulder cords. Honda drives Tonbokiri, the spear named for the legend of a dragonfly landing on its blade and falling away in two pieces, deep into the Predator's back. The grip loosens for half a second. Ieyasu drops out of his breastplate like shedding a coat, rips the cracked mask off, and leaves the empty armor in the hunter's hands.

Everyone runs for an old stone well, and Honda shoves Ieyasu in first. The franchise never fixed the bomb's size, but Predator 2 puts a number on the first film's blast: enough rainforest to cover 300 city blocks. Dutch survived that blast. The flash travels in straight lines, so everyone below the lip of the well sits in its shadow. A well is a foxhole with stone walls, and the US Army's nuclear protection manual (FM 3-4) says digging in provides the best nuclear defense.

The blast wave flattens the shrine and snaps the hilltop trees, and the well shatters, but the men inside survive. Where the main hall stood there is a glowing crater with no body, no blades and not even Ieyasu's breastplate, because the device is built to leave nothing behind. Ieyasu climbs out with the cracked mask and his portrait. Tier F: the hunter is gone, and every earlier hunt had already cost it its eyes, cloak and mask.

What it comes down to

The Predator has the better weapons, but terrain decides each hunt. Ieyasu learned at Mikatagahara that the stronger side wins by choosing the ground, and he spends the night choosing it: fire to blind thermal vision, water to short the cloak, paper screens to reveal the shimmer, and a stone well to shield against the bomb. Each trap uses a real weakness from the films, so the tier drops from A to B to D to F until nothing is left of the hunter but a cracked mask.
