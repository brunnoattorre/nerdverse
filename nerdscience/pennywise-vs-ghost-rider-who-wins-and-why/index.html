Pennywise vs Ghost Rider: Who Wins and Why

Ghost Rider hunts Pennywise through Derry's sewers, its illusions, a dream and the Deadlights, testing whether hellfire and the Penance Stare can beat a fear eater.

Ghost Rider walks out of Derry, but Pennywise survives by running away.

Every NerdScience fight

The Sewer Manifestation

B

Ghost RiderGhost Rider vs Pennywise (a fragment, in the Derry sewers)

Ghost Rider drops into the storm drains under Jackson Street and meets a fragment of the thing, not the whole of it. The book lets It show up as different shapes to different witnesses at the same moment, so a fragment is no stretch. The fragment is fast and it laughs at pain. The chain answers to Johnny's thoughts, so it wraps a bloated arm before the swing finishes, and the flesh underneath burns.

The shotgun hit is nine .33-caliber pellets at about 1,300 feet per second, delivered as hellfire. The hole closes in under three seconds. Human skin closes at a fraction of a millimeter a day. The regeneration is millions of times faster, and it doesn't stop the fire from landing.

The one stumble comes when the face under the paint turns into Crash Simpson. In Marvel Spotlight #5, Johnny sold his soul to Mephisto to save Crash, who died anyway. Reading fear and wearing it is the oldest rule in this thing's canon. The chain goes slack for half a second because Johnny hesitates, not the Spirit.

The Spirit takes the wheel back and wins on volume. Hellfire burns the soul and body together, so it doesn't heal like an ordinary wound. Chain wrap after chain wrap leaves the fragment no room to reform between hits. It can't hold Crash's face and knit its skin at the same time, so the mask collapses into the sewage. We grade it B because Rider won on raw output without understanding what he's fighting, and his hands won't stop shaking.

Derry Bends the Rules

A

Ghost RiderGhost Rider vs Pennywise (Derry's illusions, the Kitchener Ironworks)

Our reasoning rests on the book tying It to Derry so tightly that the town is part of the monster. Pennywise has a psychic hold on the whole town, and the book never shows that hold reaching past the town line. Streets folding and drains exhaling are its home field, not a new power. The chain swings through empty air because the spot the shape stood in has already been rewritten. The problem is targeting, not strength.

Then the town drops Johnny into a borrowed memory: the Kitchener Ironworks explosion on Easter Sunday, 1906, in the middle of a children's egg hunt. In the novel it killed 102 people, 88 of them kids. Johnny was never there. The amygdala flags a threat in under a fifth of a second and doesn't check whose memory it came from. For a heartbeat, the illusion works on the man instead of the Spirit.

Then Johnny notices it's all stage dressing built from fear. The book's one hard rule is that whatever shape It takes, it obeys that shape's laws. In 1958 the Losers hurt a werewolf with silver because they believed silver hurts werewolves, and Eddie's inhaler worked like acid because he believed it was acid. The shapes run on the fear of whoever's looking. The 2019 film reduces this to insulting the clown until it shrinks.

So he stops swinging at the clown and slams a burning hand into the floor of the false ironworks. This part is our own reading: the comics test hellfire on souls, and in Derry the town's soul is the thing under it. Hellfire pours into the fear soaked into the ground, the illusion flickers like a bad TV signal, and Pennywise recoils for the first time. That crack in the illusion earns the A.

Hunting the Hunter

Contested: neither side lands a finishing blowGhost Rider's Penance Stare vs Pennywise (a fear eater)

On paper this is a matchup between a predator and the one animal built to starve it. The Penance Stare forces a target to feel all the pain it ever caused innocents at once. Pennywise eats terror, and in the book fear salts the meat. Terror is a body event, with adrenaline pushing a resting pulse of about 70 past 115 in seconds, and that spike is what It tastes. Pennywise sees the danger and stops trying to outfight Johnny. It tries to outplace him.

It yanks him into the dream, a place stitched from generations of Derry's dead kids, with balloons and drains on streets with no address. The stare needs eye contact, and you can't lock eyes with a place. The Rider keeps returning to the real tunnel under Jackson Street by following his idling engine, the one thing in Derry that isn't afraid. Hellfire needs a target that exists. Burn a nightmare and you get a brighter nightmare.

A piece of a child's fear latches onto Johnny's shoulder to feed on his guilt, and he burns it off the moment he feels the weight. Then we check the stare's record, and it's ugly. Centurious walked through it because he had sold his soul and had nothing left to burn. The 2007 Ghost Rider film ran the same rule on Blackheart, who had no soul to burn. A zealot named Deacon in Jason Aaron's run laughed it off, and the Cosmic Ghost Rider's stare found nothing to punish in baby Thanos, who was still innocent. The stare doesn't run on pain. It runs on the target having something to feel it over.

Nothing in fiction cares less than the thing under Derry. Ghost Rider can't pin Pennywise long enough to lock eyes, and Pennywise can't hold him dream-side long enough to feed. So we call it contested and give this round no tier.

Full Power Collision

A

Ghost RiderGhost Rider vs the Deadlights (Pennywise's true form)

Pennywise drops every mask and pulls from the whole town at once. In the book the true form is the Deadlights, a thing from outside the universe that landed on this ground ages ago. The spider is just the closest shape a human brain can hold. The chain still works because it follows thought, not aim. It tightens on whatever is there, and the thing can't pick a shape fast enough to slip the loop.

Then Johnny locks the stare on it, and it fails exactly as Chapter 3 predicted. The pain of a thousand children is the menu to a fear eater. The finisher bounces off, but eye contact runs both ways, and the thing behind the eyes is the Deadlights. In the book that means insanity or death. Audra comes back catatonic, and Beverly goes limp and floats in the 2017 film. Johnny is human, so the light goes in and he goes out like a pilot light.

That is what flips the fight. In Ghost Rider vol. 6 #13, during the World War Hulk tie-in, Doctor Strange says the Rider's power is limitless and only restricted by Johnny Blaze's consciousness. The Deadlights just took Johnny out of the way. What's left is the Spirit, unrestricted, holding a chain wrapped around a body.

The iron works rule finishes it: this shape obeys its shape's laws, and the shape is flesh. Hellfire runs down the chain into the flesh and whatever is under it. The book says what It does when hurt: it retreats. It ran from seven children and went to sleep early. So it runs here too, down into the crack it came up from. We give it an A because the finisher failed but Rider held the ground, and Pennywise is banished, not erased.

What it comes down to

We decide every round by whether Derry's rules can touch a target. Hellfire beats the fragment on volume and beats the illusions by burning the fear-soaked ground. The Penance Stare fails because Pennywise has never regretted a meal. The Deadlights then knock out Johnny Blaze, which frees the Spirit of Vengeance from its one limit. The Rider hurts the thing so badly it flees, so he survives, but Pennywise isn't killed, only paused for another 27 years.
