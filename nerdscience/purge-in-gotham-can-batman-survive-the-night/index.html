Purge in Gotham: Can Batman Survive the Night?

Batman has to get Gotham to sunrise through a 12-hour Purge, with Arkham emptied, the cops locked in and his no-kill rule under pressure.

Batman gets Gotham to sunrise with his no-kill rule intact, scoring a B+.

Every NerdScience fight

The Triage

B

Batman and NightwingBatman and Nightwing vs Victor Zsasz and the Arkham escapees

The block falls to geometry, not firepower. The escapees come with shotguns, hunting rifles and a pistol in every second hand, and Batman can't walk through that. A rifle round can leave the barrel at around 2,700 feet per second, more than twice the speed of sound. Handgun-rated Level IIIA armor stops up to a .44 Magnum, but rifle rounds go straight through it. That is why Lucius Fox's spec sheet in Batman Begins says the suit stops anything but a straight shot.

Nightwing makes it work. He rolls a dumpster across the mouth of the alley until the gap is barely two people wide, then fires two flash charges. The stampede jams on itself, shoulders pin arms, and nobody can aim. Batman works down the line with elbows, batarangs and zip cuffs, and Zsasz goes last. Zsasz is the Batman villain who carves a tally mark into his own skin for every victim. Here a batarang pins his knife hand and a grapnel line takes his ankles.

Red Hood has a clean line on Zsasz from two streets over, and tonight nobody would charge him. Jason Todd is the Robin readers voted to kill by phone in 1988. He came back in 2005 arguing Batman should have killed the Joker. Batman says no once and Red Hood holds his fire. The block ends with no deaths, which earns a B. Gotham General has no open beds, and every fight Batman wins is a fight he isn't having somewhere else.

The Holiday

C

Black Mask keeps the district; Batman saves Gotham GeneralBatman vs Black Mask, with the Joker's ambulance raid on the hospitals

Black Mask doesn't beat Batman in a fair fight. Roman Sionis is built in the comics as an organizer, a rich kid who carved his mask from his father's ebony coffin. So he fights inside a gunfight, in his own club on Park Row. He swings a steel chair into Batman's ribs while Penguin's crew shoots through the windows. Then he strips the advantage away. A batarang takes his pistol, but he already has a knife, and he drives Batman back over a pool table.

The deciding hold is a forearm on the arteries of the neck. That's a blood choke, and studies put the average at about nine seconds to lights out. Oracle's call about the hospital lobby cuts in just in time. Batman gets one boot against the table and shoves. Black Mask is wide open for half a second, and that half second is the whole choice. Finishing him means being stuck there, cuffing him and dodging crossfire. Batman leaves, and the district falls within the hour.

He leaves because Gotham General is a single point of failure. An adult carries about a gallon and a third of blood, losing roughly 40% of it means life-threatening shock, and a bad wound can kill in about five minutes. The wounded get carried to the nearest doctors, so the hospital pulls in whoever is chasing them. Barbara Gordon is already inside, and the Joker shot and paralyzed her in The Killing Joke in 1988. She holds the maternity-wing hallway with a baton in each hand until the Batmobile comes through the lobby. We score it a C because the hospital held only because Oracle was already standing there.

Hour Six

B-

Batman's enclave planBatman, Oracle and the family vs the gangs and the Arkham mob

Batman never plans to put the fire out. A man who only fights fires can't beat a city burning in 40 places at once, so he starves it. Oracle cuts the lights where the gangs need them, including Penguin's warehouses and Black Mask's club. Cell towers have backup batteries that last a few hours, so she knocks them offline. Walkie-talkies don't need the grid, so she buries their channels in static. Organized violence runs on knowing who is where, and crews that guess run into each other. Penguin's men end up shooting at shapes that turn out to be Black Mask's.

It's the same playbook as 1999's No Man's Land. Gotham is cut off, the gangs carve it into turf, and Batman takes it back sector by sector with Oracle on the inside. Then Lucius Fox's barriers come online, gray panels bolted to overpasses months ago under a contract nobody questioned. Each one works like a real police Long Range Acoustic Device, which can reach up to about 160 dB at one meter, a little over three feet. Pain starts around 120 to 130 dB, and every 10 dB is ten times the energy. So 160 carries 1,000 to 10,000 times the energy of the pain threshold, and the beams point outward, away from the civilians behind them.

Gotham becomes a ring of enclaves, each small enough for one member of the family to anchor. Batman takes the biggest, the Park Row blocks around the Monarch Theater, with nearly 9,000 people in the basement and garages. The mob hits the barrier at about 3 a.m. Hands clamp over ears, and loud sound also disturbs the inner-ear balance organ, so knees buckle. It causes unsteadiness and occasional nausea, not vomiting. Batman holds the one gap with a batarang, a grapnel and a knee, never anything that kills. The crowd realizes nobody is coming to help, and the wave breaks. We score it a B-, because he is bleeding through his suit.

6:59 a.m.

B+

Batman, with the no-kill rule intactBatman vs the Joker and his clown crew at St. Jude's

By hour 11 Batman is running on empty. His muscles and liver store roughly 1,800 to 2,000 calories of glycogen, and hard effort burns through it in 90 minutes to two hours. The body clears half of any adrenaline dose in about two minutes, so it keeps dumping fresh ones, and the side effect is the tremor in his hands. The comics show where this ends. In 1993's Knightfall, Bane empties Arkham, lets Batman wear himself out rounding everyone up, then breaks his back in Batman #497. St. Jude's is the one hospital no enclave covers, and Batman is the only one close enough.

Two things keep him standing. In most versions the Joker doesn't want Batman dead. In The Dark Knight he says as much: without Batman, what would he do? So tonight's order is that nobody shoots the bat. And the corridor is the same bottleneck Nightwing used in Crime Alley, so only two clowns can reach him at a time. He catches an axe handle and shoves its owner into the next two. A pipe puts him on one knee, but he gets up.

Buckshot leaves the barrel at about 1,300 feet per second and crosses the hall in a few hundredths of a second. Average human reaction time is a quarter of a second. So Batman doesn't dodge the shot. He reads the barrel coming up and is already moving when it fires, and the blast chews the wall where his head was. The shotgun lands at his feet and killing is legal, but he doesn't pick it up. At seven the siren sounds and the clowns freeze. The Joker lowers his gun too, because his joke needed Batman to kill while it was legal. That window just closed.

Oracle's count is around 900 dead. The Narrows and half the Bowery fell, though every enclave held. For scale, the 1992 Los Angeles riots killed more than 50 people over six days with police and the National Guard on the streets. We score the night a B+. The rule held, and the enclaves held because four other people split the load. But the last minute wasn't Batman's win alone. The Joker had the shot and chose the joke.

What it comes down to

Batman can't win a Purge by fighting. He wins it by shrinking the city. Bottlenecks, a blackout, sound barriers and four allies each holding an enclave get Gotham to sunrise. His no-kill rule survives the one minute when it would have been easiest to break, at a cost of about 900 lives. He ends at a B+ because the rule held and the plan held, but the last minute depended on the Joker.
