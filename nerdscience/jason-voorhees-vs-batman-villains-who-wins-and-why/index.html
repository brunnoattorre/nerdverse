Jason Voorhees vs Batman Villains: Who Wins and Why

Jason Voorhees washes up in Gotham and takes on the Joker, Mr. Freeze, Deathstroke and Clayface, one fight at a time, while Batman trails him.

Only Mr. Freeze's ice stopped Jason, and Freeze thawed him himself.

Every NerdScience fight

The Joker

B

Jason VoorheesJason Voorhees vs the Joker

The laughing gas gets the Joker nowhere. Jason walks through the funhouse door while the canisters hiss and never coughs or slows. That isn't drug immunity. In Freddy vs. Jason, two syringes of tranquilizer put him on the floor. But Joker Venom, the Joker's signature since 1940, is a gas, and a gas only works if you breathe it. Jason spent seven years chained at the bottom of Crystal Lake, and the movies never say he breathes. We're betting he doesn't, so the gas never gets in.

Next the goons open fire. Rifle rounds carrying roughly 1,300 to 1,600 joules each hit his chest and he keeps walking, the same way he shrugged off a sheriff's shotgun and revolver in Jason Lives. Then comes the Joker's best card: a spring-loaded spike that fires up through Jason's thigh and pins him to the boardwalk. Jason stops, and the Joker can't resist skipping in to tap the mask with his joy buzzer.

That's the mistake. A normal man's spinal cord yanks a speared leg away before the brain gets a vote. Jason's leg never jerks. He pulls the plank up, tears out of the boardwalk with the spike still through him, and grabs the Joker by the collar. The strength that took a man's head off in one punch in Jason Takes Manhattan drives the Joker back through a wall of funhouse mirrors. The machete opens his shoulder to the bone, and he drops down a sewer grate.

The trap needed Jason to care about a spike, and the bow needed the Joker within reach. Chaos wants an audience, a laugh, a scream, anything. Jason just wants an end, and the Joker has nothing to say to that. We score it a B because the damage was real: a deep laceration across the ribs from mirror glass, a crack through the edge of the mask, and the spike left in his thigh. He never changed pace.

Mr. Freeze

F

Mr. Freeze freezes Jason solid, then thaws him and loses the fightJason Voorhees vs Mr. Freeze

Victor Fries goes through the pier footage and the autopsy notes and builds a theory: regeneration isn't free. A resting human body puts out about 100 watts of heat just staying alive, and rebuilding tissue costs more. Pull heat out faster than Jason makes it and even he runs out of fuel. Fries has the motive too. Heart of Ice, the 1992 Batman: The Animated Series episode, gave him Nora, a dying wife in a cryo chamber. He also knows Jason X, where the government kept Jason around to study how he heals.

The trap is a decommissioned cold storage warehouse with steel walls 3 feet thick and one way in. Jason follows the sound of violence through the loading door and it seals behind him. The cryo cannon frosts his right arm white, and he keeps walking with ice snapping off his shoulders. Then he grabs the catwalk rail with his frozen hand and tears a whole section off its bolts. Freeze drops to one knee 10 feet above a man who needs one more step.

So Freeze floods the room with liquid nitrogen mist. It boils at -320°F, and the warehouse dives toward that. His metabolism math is wrong: Jason, a corpse that lightning got back on its feet in Jason Lives, has no metabolism to starve. But ice doesn't care whether you're alive. Water expands about 9% when it freezes, and crystals grow inside and between cells and lock every joint. Jaw, knees and the hand on the rail all seize, and Jason stops. Jason X is the precedent: a cryopod froze him in 2010 and he didn't move until 2455, 445 years later.

Freeze's math was wrong and his method was right. Nora is waiting and he needs to watch a body restart, so he warms Jason a few degrees at a time. In Jason X, a student had that job and Jason froze her face in liquid nitrogen. Jason comes back faster than Freeze's model predicts, because it assumed a body that runs on heat. His fist rips out the suit's power coil, and Freeze, who must stay below zero to live, starts warming and flees to the harbor. We score it an F, and it isn't a typo. Jason didn't survive that fight. Freeze let him go.

Deathstroke

A

Jason Voorhees (Deathstroke walks away from the contract)Jason Voorhees vs Deathstroke

Slade Wilson scouts Jason through a rifle scope for an hour. He sees no tactics and no fear response: Jason doesn't check corners, duck for cover or look up. Slade opens with a clean head shot from about a quarter mile. The round clips the curve of the mask and tears off a white flake, and Jason doesn't turn his head. So Slade comes down and fights him like a soldier: a blade behind the knee, two fingers snapped back, pressure points along the neck that drop grown men. Every strike lands clean and nothing changes.

Jason's machete swings are wide and heavy, and Slade slips every one. But Slade escalates. He leads Jason into the railyard, blows a gas main so the corner of the lot goes up, sets off breaching charges along a fence line, and drops a shipping container, about 5,000 pounds of steel, from a crane onto Jason's left leg. A pinned target, a burning yard and every weapon he owns is Slade's best moment. An explosion kills three ways: the pressure wave, shrapnel and heat. About 5 psi starts bursting eardrums and around 15 tears lungs. A normal man is dead three times over.

Slade walks into the smoke to confirm the kill, and Jason is already moving. He drags the leg out from under the container, and the kneecap doesn't come out in one piece. The machete comes out of the smoke, the one thing Slade's reflexes can't read, and opens his ribs. His healing factor will close the cut. That isn't what stops him. His whole career assumes the enemy wants to live and that a man hurt badly enough backs off. Jason isn't part of that market. Slade empties every gun he has into Jason's chest, and every round does nothing. He says it aloud: there's no winning condition.

A bigger bomb wouldn't change it. In Jason Goes to Hell, an FBI team riddles Jason with bullets and blows him to pieces, and it works. Then his still-beating heart hypnotizes the coroner into eating it, and Jason keeps going in a borrowed body. Destroy the body and the problem just moves. So Slade walks away. The one contract that famously came apart on him was the Judas Contract against the Teen Titans in 1984. We score it an A: Jason took the heaviest damage so far and kept advancing, but with a shattered knee, a chunk of shoulder gone and burns up his neck and back, he's visibly slower.

Clayface

S

Jason VoorheesJason Voorhees vs Clayface

Clayface has watched every fight. Bullets and blades go through him, he rebuilds from any wound, and he's sure he can't be frozen or burned. He plans to outlast Jason by being just as durable and a lot smarter, and he picks the ground himself: the old Gotham Clay Works, a flooded quarry full of rusted machinery and open furnaces. He's wrong about himself. In Arkham City, Batman beats him with freeze grenades. Cold hurts that body, the chemistry of clay says heat will be worse, and he just picked a building full of furnaces.

His first exchange goes well. The machete sinks into his chest like mud, and he closes around the blade and holds it. His arm swells into a spiked mace and slams Jason into a steel wall that folds on top of him, crushing Jason's left forearm. Then Clayface splits, the Arkham City trick where he makes clay thugs to fight for him. Five bodies rush in and pile on heavy wet clay. Clay punches through Jason's chest in three places, and they drag the whole mass into the quarry water for about a minute.

It's the best plan anyone has all night, and it lands on Jason's best ground. He spent seven years at the bottom of Crystal Lake, and you can't drown something that doesn't need to breathe. He walks along the bottom and climbs out with Clayface still clinging to him. Then he does what he does in movie after movie and uses whatever's in the room: liquid nitrogen in Jason X, and tonight a furnace.

The chemistry pays off here. Heat clay past 1,022°F and the water chemically bound inside it is driven off for good, so it can never go soft again. Heat wet clay too fast and the water flashes to steam and cracks it apart. Jason walks into the open furnace with Clayface wrapped around him. Clayface's outer layers flash to steam and split, the mass underneath bakes, and nothing answers when he tries to reshape. He falls off Jason in dry, cracked shards that never stand up again. We score it an S: the hardest fight Jason won, and the room won it for him.

What it comes down to

Three of Gotham's worst couldn't stop Jason, because the Joker's gas, Deathstroke's weapons and Clayface's crush and drowning all assume a man who breathes, flinches and wants to live. Mr. Freeze got closest, not with metabolism math but with plain ice, the same way Jason X held him for 445 years. Then Freeze opened the freezer because Nora is still waiting in one. Clayface fell to a furnace, and Batman is left holding a machete tip packed in dry ice.
