Hulk vs Doomsday: Who Wins and Why

Three versions of Hulk fight the same Doomsday, the monster that killed Superman, and each round earns a survival tier from F to S.

Doomsday beats all three Hulks, then lets Banner live.

Every NerdScience fight

MCU Hulk

D

DoomsdayMCU Hulk vs Doomsday

Hulk opens the way he flattened Loki in The Avengers. He covers the ground in two strides, lands three punches, grabs Doomsday by the leg and slams him left, right, left. For about ten seconds it looks easy. Then Doomsday stands up without a mark. Loki stayed down. This thing doesn't.

The problem is what he's hitting. This Hulk once stopped a charging Chitauri Leviathan with one punch, but a Leviathan is an armored animal. Doomsday is about 7 feet and 615 lbs of almost solid mass with no internal organs, so there's nothing in there to rupture. Newton's third law does the rest. When the target won't give, the damage lands in the hand that threw the punch. Doomsday catches Hulk's fourth swing by the wrist, and when Hulk pulls, nothing moves.

Then the spikes. Doomsday doesn't carry weapons, it grows them. Its body is armored in bone, and it can push new spikes out on command. A bone blade slides out of its forearm and goes through Hulk's side until the tip shows on the other side. This Hulk does heal, but the next hit reopens the fracture in his forearm before it closes. Doomsday throws him through the arena wall, and his best counterpunch does nothing.

Hulk's record against real heavyweights doesn't help. He beat Thor in Ragnarok only because the man running the arena rigged it. Thanos took him apart in the opening of Infinity War, and Hulk refused to come out for most of the movie. So he backs away, and Doomsday lets him go. That's the 1992 pattern: it broke the Justice League but finished none of them, and Blue Beetle ended up in a coma, not a grave. We score it a D. He's alive only because Doomsday was done with him.

Comics Hulk

DoomsdayComics Hulk vs Doomsday

This Hulk is smaller than you'd picture. Marvel's Handbook lists Classic Hulk at 7 to 8 feet and 1,040 to 1,400 lbs, about Doomsday's height but 400 to 800 lbs heavier. What he adds is 60-plus years of feats. He's traded blows with Thor for decades, and in 1984's Secret Wars he held up a 150-billion-ton mountain range so it wouldn't crush his teammates. His strength also climbs exponentially with his anger, with no limit named, and he heals faster the angrier he gets.

For a while he wins. His first swing drives Doomsday into the ground hard enough to leave a crater. He caves in the bone plating on its chest like a crushed soda can. He snaps a spike off its shoulder. Then he slams Doomsday through a mountain and brings the mountain down behind it.

Watch what happens each time Doomsday gets up. The caved chest has healed thicker. The snapped spike grows back sharper. The same punches that were breaking it open land a little softer each round. Hulk isn't getting weaker. The target is getting harder.

Here's the mechanism. Things break from pressure, which is force divided by the area it lands on. Spread a hit over twice the area and the pressure at any one point halves. Real bone does a slow version of this under Wolff's law, rebuilding itself over a long stretch to handle the loads it carries. Doomsday does it between punches, so thicker plating means more area and less pressure from the same punch. It's an inference, and we say so. But canon shows Doomsday growing bone mid-fight. In Hunter/Prey it sealed its own ear shut to beat Superman's ultrasonic weapon. In the 1992 fight, Superman noticed it getting stronger as they went.

Hulk misses all of it. He sees Doomsday on its back and brings double-fisted hammer blows down on its skull until its body goes slack. His scream sets off avalanches three ridges away. For a second he's done what Superman did in 1992. Then Doomsday's hand locks around his throat. It was built to come back from every death immune to whatever killed it, and being beaten to death is the one thing it has already survived. Fists are the only weapon a Hulk has. Doomsday pins him by the throat and pounds him until the clawing stops, then drops him in the crater and walks away, same as the Justice League and MCU Hulk.

World Breaker Hulk

B

Doomsday (but Hulk wins the first exchanges)World Breaker Hulk vs Doomsday

The name comes from a prophecy on the planet Sakaar, and he lived up to it at the end of 2007's World War Hulk, when his stomps sent tremors hundreds of miles and threatened to shatter the planet. Doomsday goes in low and fast at the joints: the knee, the elbow, the seam under the ribs. Bone spikes rake across them and Hulk buckles for half a second. Then he swings. The punch lands on Doomsday's chest and a shockwave flattens the arena and peels topsoil off the land for miles. Doomsday goes through a mountain range and a coastline, then skips across an ocean like a flat stone. It's the first exchange Doomsday loses.

'Planet-cracking' gets thrown around loosely, so here's the scale. To blow Earth apart for good, so gravity can't pull the pieces back together, you'd have to beat its gravitational binding energy, about 2 x 10^32 joules, roughly a week of the Sun's entire output. In World War Hulk he only threatened it. But in Incredible Hulks #634 he admitted he'd been holding back even then. And the one time he went all out in a dimension with nobody innocent in it, he shattered the planet he stood on.

Doomsday doesn't flinch from that. Its Kryptonian hunting instinct was a retcon two years after 1992, when it ignored Superman whenever it could. In Hunter/Prey it took Darkseid's eyebeams at full power and beat him anyway. The first time anyone killed fully grown Doomsday, it took an energy blast that laid waste to a fifth of the planet. When Hulk catches a punch and snaps its forearm clean through, the arm knits back together in a second. When he slams both fists into its skull, the ground liquefies and Doomsday's knees bend.

So Hulk throws the biggest punch of his life, and Doomsday steps inside it and grows a spike out of its fist, the same trick it used on MCU Hulk. Both hits land at once. Hulk's fist caves Doomsday's chest in, and the spike goes through his chest and out his back. That's how Superman #75 ends, with two blows landing at the same time and both fighters going down. Tonight only one of them is built to shrug it off, and Doomsday's chest fills back in. Hitting it harder buys time, not a win.

Hulk drops to one knee, and we grade the round at that moment. B. He hits harder than anything Doomsday has faced tonight, and he's the first Hulk to buckle its knees. That B is his grade only if he stays down. He doesn't.

The Final Exchange

D

DoomsdayWorld Breaker Hulk vs Doomsday

Hulk tears the arm out of his chest and hauls himself upright while magma crawls up through the cracks. The hole in his chest is already closing. He throws everything he has left, right, left, and for five punches Doomsday goes backward. It's the last ground it gives all night. By the sixth, Doomsday is shifting its stance and bracing at the exact angle the next fist will arrive. Each blow lands duller than the last.

This decides the night. Hulk's strength may have no ceiling, but his healing can run dry. Every spike Doomsday drives in opens a wound, and when wounds open faster than they close, the gap only grows. So Doomsday opens them. Spikes punch out of its knuckles, elbows and knees, and every one finds Hulk. Ten wounds, then twenty. Doomsday doesn't need to eat, drink, breathe or sleep.

Hulk also has a documented off switch. At the end of World War Hulk, his fight with the Sentry burned them both back into ordinary men, and Tony Stark's satellites knocked him down to Bruce Banner. Doomsday has no off switch. Hulk's last punch is the angriest he has left, and it turns Doomsday's head about 3 inches. Then the head turns back. His healing falls behind, then gives out, and the green drains away. The giant folds down into a 128-lb physicist lying in the middle of the arena.

Doomsday stands over Bruce Banner and its hand opens, then closes. The bird in the opening is real canon. It's Doomsday's first kill in Superman: The Man of Steel #18, where a bird lands in its hand and it crushes the bird. So which Doomsday shows up? The one that crushed the bird, or the one that walked past a beaten Justice League? Canon doesn't settle it, so we call it. Banner is a beaten opponent, not a bird, and that rule held for both Hulks before him. Doomsday looks down for a long moment, then walks. We score it a D. He out-hit Doomsday, and he's alive for the same reason MCU Hulk is: Doomsday was done with him.

What it comes down to

No Hulk loses to Doomsday because Doomsday is stronger. He loses because all he has is his fists, and being beaten to death is the one thing Doomsday has already come back from. Every punch makes the next Hulk's job harder. Even World Breaker Hulk, who hits hardest and buckles Doomsday's knees, runs out of healing before Doomsday runs out of spikes. All three survive only because Doomsday walks away from a beaten opponent, as it did with the Justice League. The best grade of the night is World Breaker Hulk's B, and he spends it standing back up.
