Justice League vs Doctor Doom: Who Wins and Why

Doctor Doom binds Superman with a coven's spell and hits Metropolis on five fronts, and we test whether the rest of the Justice League can win it back.

The Justice League wins, Tier B: they break the binding, then Superman beats Doom.

Every NerdScience fight

The Bind

A

Doom's coven binds SupermanSuperman vs Doom's army and coven

Superman reaches the front line first and nothing the army has touches him. A modern tank round leaves the barrel at about a mile a second and bounces off his chest. Heat vision melts rifles, and for 90 seconds it looks like the invasion ends before the local news can cut to a live shot. His mistake is writing off the chanting as theater. The soldiers were never the attack.

Doom doesn't reach for kryptonite, because he already tried that. In the 1981 Superman and Spider-Man crossover, Doom hit Superman with a kryptonite ray, and Superman rolled himself in lead torn from Doom's own lead-lined floor to get rid of it. So Doom picks the one thing lead can't stop. Magic isn't kryptonite. It doesn't poison Superman. It ignores what makes him Superman and hits him the way it would hit you.

The binding targets the biology. Superman's cells soak up yellow sunlight and store it like a battery, and Superman Unchained #2 puts the intake at roughly 140 gigawatts. Every punch, flight and freeze breath is spent from that charge. The coven isn't attacking his muscle. It's attacking the charger.

The effects come in order. Flight fails first, and he drops from 30 feet and cracks the asphalt at about 30 mph. A tank round that should bounce leaves a bruise, and heat vision flickers out. Doom stops his riflemen anyway, since a dead Superman teaches him nothing. We give it a Tier A because the League is intact and Superman is alive, with Batman already looking for what holds the spell together.

Five Fronts

B

Doom's prep wears the League downWonder Woman, Flash, Green Lantern, Aquaman and Batman vs Doom's generals, sigils, artillery and fleet

Wonder Woman wins every exchange with Doom's generals on Fifth Avenue. Her sword, shield and lasso put the first three down. Then Doom steps in and one green bolt rings her shield like a church bell and slides her back 20 feet. He isn't faster than her. Hermes gave her speed, and she bats away rifle rounds. What he has is a force field, and a rule that his armor can't attack without dropping the field. Every bolt opens a window, and she finds one and drives her sword into his chest plate.

Doom's answer is to aim the next bolt at the crowd behind her. She blocks it, then the next one. He doesn't need to outfight her, only to keep her choosing civilians over him, and she always will. Every block costs her another yard of street.

Two miles north, the Flash clears the ritual's supply depot of candles, vials and iron charms in about four seconds. Then gold sigils snap across the pavement and up the walls, because Doom knows he runs up buildings. Speed can't help here. The Flash can outreact anything that's trying to keep up with him, but a trap isn't trying. It sits there, and the faster he runs, the more of it he steps on.

Green Lantern holds a dome over six city blocks while 155 mm shells (about 6 inches wide, 95 lb each) pound it. No single shell matters. The charge does, because a ring runs on willpower but holds a finite charge, and lanterns have long had to recharge every 24 hours. Aquaman has the best day, since a wave from his trident folds Doom's destroyers, but every minute at the harbor is a minute he isn't with the others. We score it B: they hurt Doom's army, but the binding eats every advantage.

The Engineered Spell

C

The League breaks the bindingBatman and the League vs Doom's binding machine

Batman finds a wiring diagram in Doom's command tent: amplifier stages, signal paths and a crystal cut to one exact frequency. Doom built a machine to carry the binding and taught the coven to sing into it, since a spell this big needs someone singing all day and he has a war to run. That gives the spell a body. Break the crystal and nothing is left for it to run through.

It's very Doom. His origin is a machine too. He built one in college to reach his mother's soul in hell, and Reed Richards told him the math was off. It blew up in his face. Batman uses the same physics your wristwatch does. Quartz cut to vibrate 32,768 times a second rings at that frequency and nothing else, and anything that broadcasts leaves a trail. He tunes a receiver and takes two bearings a quarter mile apart. They cross at the old clock tower, which stopped at 9:14 because something underneath it switched on.

Batman calls everyone in. Flash stops running from the sigils and reads them, studying the pattern at his speed until he finds the one lane Doom couldn't cover. Wonder Woman, Aquaman and Green Lantern push in behind him. Then the guard line closes, the emerald walls buckle with the ring near single digits, and Superman's face hits the pavement. That's the League's lowest point and why this is a C.

Wonder Woman gets one step closer than the line allows. Her blade comes down on the machine's casing and the crystal cracks like breaking ice. The singing cuts off mid-note. The spell was never the coven alone. It was the machine under a landmark they could see from every rooftop.

Superman vs Doom

B

SupermanSuperman (with the League) vs Doctor Doom

The power comes back like a dam breaking, and the League closes ranks around Superman. Doom says he wanted to see him at full strength. It's part ego and part logic. If the binding held, Doom owned Metropolis. If it broke, he got Superman up close to study, which is why he stopped the rifles. Superman throws the first punch and pulls it, because there's a man inside that armor. It lands on Doom's field, which reaches 8 feet out, and Doom skids back 50 feet inside his own bubble.

For a while Doom wins the trade. He drops the field, fires green sorcery point-blank, and magic still doesn't care who Superman is. Superman is pulling punches and Doom isn't pulling spells. The armor doesn't change this. It's titanium, nuclear-powered, forged in a Tibetan monastery and upgraded to shrug off the Human Torch. That's great armor, not god armor.

Superman figures out that the tempo is the trap and stops trading. The deciding rule is the one Wonder Woman found: Doom can't attack without dropping his field. Human reaction time is about a quarter of a second, and Doom has never been fast, only prepared. Superman waits for the next bolt and hits the same spot on the chest plate 15 times in that window, every punch pulled. The 15th goes through, the field never comes back up, and Doom goes through the clock tower's stone base.

Heat vision is the wrong finisher. Titanium melts at about 3,000°F, but this armor was built for the Human Torch and has a reactor inside. Superman uses freeze breath instead, and Doom's gauntlets are frozen to the rubble before he can raise them. Wonder Woman's lasso goes around his shoulders. Doom prepared for Superman's strength, not for Superman plus a team that bought him the time to use it. We score it B: the League walks out, but every hero is bleeding and a dozen blocks of downtown are rubble.

What it comes down to

Doom's plan was to take Superman off the board with a binding that drains his solar battery, then wear down the rest of the League across five fronts. The League wins because the binding had a body: a crystal machine under the clock tower that Batman tracked by its frequency and Wonder Woman cracked. After that, Superman beat Doom by exploiting the rule that Doom's armor can't attack without dropping its field. He landed 15 punches inside a quarter second, then froze Doom's gauntlets. The League wins at Tier B, with downtown in ruins, and Doom gets his long look at Superman at full power.
