The Boys vs Spider-Man Villains: Who Wins and Why

Vought bets Homelander's Seven can replace Spider-Man by beating six of his villains, and we check each marketing briefing against canon.

The Seven mostly win, but Homelander flees Carnage to protect his image.

Every NerdScience fight

A-Train vs Rhino

C

A-Train, barelyA-Train vs Rhino

A-Train throws a dozen punches in two seconds, all at Rhino's face, the one spot the armor doesn't cover. Every punch bounces. The briefing left out that it isn't just the suit: Marvel rates Rhino's skin itself against a LAW, a light anti-tank weapon. Every pound of force comes back up A-Train's arms, and by punch 12 his forearms are numb.

Rhino still shouldn't be able to grab him. A-Train's on-screen record is over 1,000 mph, around Mach 1.3, and Marvel lists Rhino's top speed at 70. But A-Train came in on the same line 12 times, and you don't need speed to catch a guy who keeps aiming at your face. A-Train's own momentum becomes Rhino's weapon. He gets swung through a support wall and pinned under the concrete.

He wriggles free and uses the oldest trick in Spider-Man's book. Rhino has been a charger since his 1966 debut, and a charge is a straight line. A-Train builds speed along the outer ramp, cuts left at the last instant, and Rhino's horn, rated to punch through 2-inch steel plate, drives into a load-bearing pillar. The ceiling folds in on him.

We score it a C because Rhino isn't beaten, only buried. Concrete is heavy stuff, so that deck is tens of tons. Rhino lifts 75 tons or more and digs out in minutes. A-Train limps away with his arm hanging wrong, holding a win on the tracker that nobody watching believes.

The Deep vs Mysterio

D

The DeepThe Deep vs Mysterio

Mysterio wins round one. Gas hisses from the ceiling vents, a hallucinogen that scrambles the brain's sense of what's real. The Deep takes about four breaths of it. Mysterio's fishbowl helmet, one-way glass with a half-hour air supply, keeps him clean. The Deep sees one shark, then six, then a giant octopus across the ceiling and Homelander drifting through the water. Every punch hits nothing.

That's the whole act. Since his 1960s debut, Mysterio has been a Hollywood effects man with zero superpowers. This version adds the drone swarm from 2019's Far From Home. He lures The Deep into a tank wall that shatters across his shoulders, then onto a floor panel that dumps a shock through the water. For one long ugly exchange, Mysterio owns him.

Then The Deep closes his eyes. The shattered tank has let a few hundred real fish into the hall, and he asks them instead. Fish have a lateral line, a row of sensors down each side that feels moving water and low-frequency vibrations at close range. A projection pushes no water. A drone does. The fish know exactly which threats are real.

They steer past sharks that aren't there and through the wake of drones that are, straight to a control rig behind a tank. The Mysterio on the catwalk was a projection all along. The real one looks up from his console as a hand closes around his ankle. The Deep drags him under and cracks the dome, and the half hour of air is gone in seconds. We score The Deep a D because he was beaten until the one power Mysterio can't fake turned it around.

Black Noir vs Doctor Octopus

B

Black NoirBlack Noir vs Doctor Octopus

The briefing calls Otto Octavius a scientist, a soft target with a harness strapped to his back, so Noir plans a quiet kill from behind. That's wrong. This is Sam Raimi's 2004 Spider-Man 2 version. The arms run their own AI. The chip that kept it in check fried in the accident, and the arms killed the hospital staff trying to cut them off while Otto was unconscious.

Noir is 3 feet away when a tentacle snaps out of the dark and plucks the blade from his hand. A human brain needs a fraction of a second to see a threat and move. The arms don't wait for that. Noir answers with the second blade and takes one arm off clean at the joint. The other three pin him to the wall by his wrists and throat.

A choke on the neck arteries drops a normal man within seconds. Noir isn't a normal man, so he gets a few more, but not many. Then he does what he does best and disappears. A smoke pellet drops from his belt, a trick straight out of the animated Diabolical, and the cloud swallows the room. Each arm sees through a single red camera lens, and now there's nothing to see.

Noir doesn't need eyes. He tracks the servo whine by ear, rolls under a swinging tentacle and drives the blade into the casing of the harness on Otto's spine, the neural link that runs every arm. The feedback runs straight into Otto's nervous system. The arms freeze mid-swing and Otto sags forward, out cold. Noir walks out with a hand on his cracked ribs. That's a B: he wins, but the soft-target briefing nearly killed him.

Starlight vs Venom

B

Starlight survives; Venom escapesStarlight vs Venom

The briefing says the symbiote hates bright light, and Starlight's whole power set is light. On paper that's a clean counter. In the fight, nobody told her how fast Venom would be. He's on her before she raises her hands, webs her wrists and throws her through an air vent. Black claws tear at her suit while Eddie Brock laughs under the snarl. She is barely surviving and she knows it.

The briefing was wrong, and it worked anyway. The symbiote's comic weaknesses are sound and heat, not light. Spider-Man shook it loose with church bells in 1984. So why does it shriek at her beam? Because it's black. Ordinary black paint soaks up the vast majority of the light that hits it and turns it into heat. Starlight isn't blinding the symbiote, she's cooking it, and it blisters and peels back from Eddie.

The rooftop keeps helping. Her beam clips an old alarm horn and a siren of 100-plus dB tears across the roof. That's sound, the other weakness. A stray shot ruptures a gas line and flame roars across the roof. The symbiote peels completely away from Eddie, but it doesn't stay gone.

With Starlight tapped out, the symbiote rebonds in a rage, bigger than before, and Venom hurls her off the edge. She can fly in the show, but only on a big charge of electricity, and she emptied the tank on that beam. She drops six floors past glass at about 40 mph before her hand catches an antenna cable. It slices her palm. She hauls herself up to an empty roof. We score it a B: she survives and Venom vanishes into the dark, but she didn't beat him.

Queen Maeve vs Green Goblin

B

Queen MaeveQueen Maeve vs Green Goblin

The briefing calls Norman Osborn unstable, a guy who throws bombs and cackles. That's half of him. The other half is a CEO who plans three moves ahead. In an unfinished 40-story skyscraper he lets Maeve chase him, drifting just out of reach. By the fourth charge he has baited her onto a pumpkin bomb that's already waiting, and the blast knocks her flat.

He knows he can't fight her up close. Marvel rates Goblin at about 9 tons of lift, and Maeve stopped a runaway armored truck in the show's first episode. So when she comes up swinging, he drops the glider three feet, lets the punch sail over his head above a 400-foot drop, and flicks a razor bat across her cheek on the way past.

That's when Maeve stops brawling. She can't fly, so she leaps, and once her feet leave the steel her arc is locked. The glider steers at up to 90 mph, so every jump lands where he used to be. She quits chasing and starts herding. She tears a loose beam free and hurls it across one side of the frame, then kicks a stack of rebar across another. Wall by wall, she shrinks the sky until Goblin has one way left to fly.

He sees it a second too late and banks for the gap anyway. Maeve is waiting on the ledge, catches the wing as it tilts and snaps it in half. Both crash through rebar two floors down. Goblin gets up first. He took his own glider through the chest in 1973 and was back in the comics in 1996, because the formula heals what should kill him. A girder lands across Maeve's legs, but a steel girder is a fraction of the weight of an armored truck, and she stops armored trucks. She shoves it off, catches his wrist before the bomb leaves his hand, squeezes until it drops, and punches him once. It's the cleanest win of the night, with the public feed dark.

Homelander vs Carnage

C

Carnage, by default: Homelander flees while winningHomelander vs Carnage

Homelander switches the public feed on for his own fight. The arena is a sealed concrete box. Carnage is one of Spider-Man's deadliest villains and a living symbiote, so he doesn't stay wounded. Heat is on the symbiote's weakness list, and Homelander has heat vision.

The beam burns a hole clean through Cletus Kasady, the man inside Carnage, and the symbiote screams as the edges of the wound smoke. Then Carnage laughs and the symbiote floods the hole with fresh red mass. Homelander punches straight through the throat patch, and tendrils knit around his fist and forearm like the hole never existed. They pin his arms, shred his sleeves (cloth, not skin) and drive him into the wall.

Carnage slashes the cape to ribbons and gets a fist around the torn cloth. The clasp that's been slipping all night finally gives, the cape tears free and Homelander drops out of the grip. The whole night turns on this second. He's free and he has the answer. He lights Carnage up at full power and it works. The symbiote shrieks, smokes, peels and keeps reforming.

How long to burn all of it away? Canon never says. Our read is minutes. That means minutes of an ugly, screaming fight live with the red light on while someone laughs at him. We've seen him face this choice. In Flight 37 in season 1, rather than attempt a messy rescue, he lets a hijacked plane crash and tells the world he got there too late. A retreat he can spin. A struggle he can't.

So he leaves the shredded cape in Carnage's fist and blasts up through the ceiling. He was winning, and it didn't matter. He isn't afraid of dying, he's afraid of being seen as mortal. We score Homelander a C. Carnage never beat him. He beat the camera.

What it comes down to

Every briefing Vought's marketing team wrote broke against canon: a slow target nobody could keep down, a light weakness that was really heat, and a soft-target scientist with AI arms. Some Seven members survive by luck and some by sharp thinking, but the last fight shows Homelander's real limit. The threat was never Carnage. It was a live feed that would show him struggling.
