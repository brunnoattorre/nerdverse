DC Heroes vs Alien: Who Lasts Longest and Why

Four DC heroes wake up in separate rooms of the Nostromo with one Xenomorph hunting them, and we score how long each survives.

Batman lasts longest by turning the cargo hold's air into the weapon.

Every NerdScience fight

Black Canary and the Medical Bay

A

Black Canary escapes through the vents; the Xenomorph survivesBlack Canary vs the Xenomorph

Dinah Lance is the only hero tonight who hurts it, because her Canary Cry works. In air, sound tops out around 194 dB, and past that a wave turns into a shock front. 5 psi of overpressure bursts eardrums and 15 psi tears lungs. A cry that shatters steel sits near the top of that scale. The first blast knocks the Xenomorph backward with its inner jaw flexing, and every glass cabinet in the room blows out.

It buys her about two seconds, and she spends them well. She drops under the tail, rolls, and puts the exam table between them. Ted Grant trained her as a boxer, and that footwork keeps her alive. A tighter second cry throws the creature into the med cart and across the room, and it cracks the carapace.

That crack is where the win becomes the problem. What comes out of a cracked Xenomorph is acid blood, and in Alien (1979) the facehugger's blood burned through deck after deck of the Nostromo. The medical bay is 10 ft by 12 ft, so no corner sits outside the splash radius. Her best weapon only works at a range where using it soaks her in something that eats steel. The cry also runs on her throat, not a battery, and her throat is already going.

The creature reads her hesitation, slides out of her firing line, and takes the wall. She doesn't trade blows. She jumps for the vent she spotted on the way in and hauls herself inside as the claws rake the tile. The shafts connect every deck, so she has a route, not a dead end. We score it an A because she is the only one who makes it bleed and she leaves on her own terms. She doesn't score higher because acid, not the creature, stopped her.

Nightwing in Engineering

B

Nightwing escapes through a floor grate; the Xenomorph survivesNightwing vs the Xenomorph

Dick Grayson follows Dinah's scream down a service ladder into three stories of pipes, catwalks and turbines. His opening is his best stretch. He vaults the first tail strike, lands on a pipe rail, and cracks the carapace with his escrima sticks, which throw blue arcs. He then uses the machinery as a playground, swinging off a valve housing for three more hits before the creature turns.

The shock is a fair fit for this ship. Electrified escrima sticks are a fairly recent Nightwing trait: the older books gave him plain batons, and the shock version starts with the Nightwing (2011) series and shows up in Batman: Arkham City too. The Nostromo crew in Alien (1979) also built electric prods to drive the creature. A prod runs thousands of volts at a few milliamps, so it hurts and does not kill. In Nightwing's hands it's a deterrent, and he knows that.

Two things cap him. Every cracked shell means standing in acid, so he can't tee off, and every exchange is shorter than he wants. And his style needs the room to cooperate. Rails, pipes and handholds are all surfaces he has to reach. The Xenomorph climbs the wall, crosses the ceiling and drops behind him, so it uses every surface he can reach and the ones he can't.

The turn comes when it cuts his grapple line with the barbed tail. He falls about 30 ft, rolls, and the tail catches his ribs. He buys time with a steam valve (350°F vapor) and then a wing-ding through a coolant line. The line flashes to freezing fog and its rupture drowns out every other vibration. The films never settle how the creature senses people, but steam, dark and clutter make it lose them, so he slips through a grate and pulls it shut. We give it a B. The coolant trick was smart, but the creature owned the ceiling.

Green Arrow in the Hangar Deck

B

Green Arrow escapes into the vents; the Xenomorph survivesGreen Arrow vs the Xenomorph

Oliver Queen walks into the biggest compartment on the ship with seven arrows. That's the character: a man with a finite number of shots who has to be right about every one. Mike Grell's 1987 miniseries, The Longbow Hunters, built that version of him. He has read the tracker and has a rule: the creature circles and climbs, and it treats the ceiling as a second floor. So he backs into a crate stack with the Narcissus shuttle on his left.

His first arrow pins the tail to a crate. His second does what everyone assumes wins the fight, and it skips off the skull. The dome is the thickest plate on the animal and it's curved, the worst geometry for a broad head. The numbers explain it. A heavy hunting bow delivers about 80 foot-pounds and a 9 mm pistol round carries closer to 380, so he can't punch through armor.

What he can do is exploit pressure, which is force over area. A thin edge goes straight through a seam. His third arrow, from 40 ft, threads the gap behind the dorsal spines and the creature screams. The hit works, and the acid it sprays eats the pinning arrow in about two seconds and frees the tail. The crate starts smoking. His best shot of the night destroys his own hardware.

After that the arithmetic turns against him. With four arrows left, then three, his shots hit plate instead of seam, and the creature stops circling and closes. A bow is made of the space between you and the target, and that space is gone. When it drops from the rafters, he dives and puts his last explosive arrow into the hydraulic ram under the loading ramp. The ramp folds, the blast throws the creature across the deck, and he drops into the vent with an empty quiver. We score it a B. He lands the cleanest hits of anyone, but acid cost him an arrow and the quiver did the rest.

Batman in the Cargo Hold

S

Batman, who vents it out of the cargo holdBatman vs the Xenomorph

Bruce Wayne doesn't move for the first three fights because he doesn't need to. He watches all of them on the ship's camera feeds like game film. He also finds the husk of the chestburster beside the dead crewman. That tells him the creature was the size of a cat this morning, is now over 7 ft, and is still growing. His read of every earlier fight is the same: each tactic worked exactly once. Dinah's cry slid out of her line, Dick's acrobatics died when it took the ceiling, and Oliver's range collapsed when it stopped circling. So he builds a place instead of bringing a tactic.

His gear is thin: batarangs, smoke capsules, a grapple gun, and a maintenance flamethrower with about 40 seconds of fuel. Fire is one of the few things this creature backs away from, which holds up in Alien (1979). He doesn't pretend a batarang does more than chip a shell. For an hour he maps the vents and picks an 8 ft lane between two crate stacks of about 10 tons each, then cuts every tie-down and mag clamp holding them. This is how he fights in the comics: arranging the ground, not out-punching anything.

The fight itself is close. He draws the creature in with an alarm on the far side of the ship. Fire redirects it into his lane, and smoke breaks its tracking. A tail hit breaks his ribs, and the flamethrower runs dry with the creature 12 ft away. He backs through a bulkhead he rigged earlier and kicks the emergency seal. The creature bends the frame trying to get through, and then he blows the cargo hatch.

The numbers decide it. A 10 ft by 10 ft hatch is 14,400 square inches, and the ship holds about 14.7 psi against it. When the seal goes, roughly 106 tons of force leaves in one direction. He cut the clamps because decompression alone doesn't work on this ship. In Alien (1979), Ripley opens the Narcissus airlock and the creature hooks the door frame and holds on. So he aims the air at the loose steel instead. Both stacks slide down the 8 ft lane with the Xenomorph standing in it. It gets a claw into the grating, the grating fails, and the hold empties in under four seconds. We give it an S because he engineered the whole fight an hour in advance.

What it comes down to

The Nostromo kills by architecture, and the ranking measures who beat the room. Black Canary hurts the Xenomorph, but acid blood stops her from following it up. Nightwing and Green Arrow land clean hits, then lose the ceiling and the quiver. Batman never beats it on strength. He puts 10 tons of steel and 106 tons of escaping air in its path before it arrives.
