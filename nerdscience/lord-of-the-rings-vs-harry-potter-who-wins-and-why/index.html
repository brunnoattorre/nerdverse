Lord of the Rings vs Harry Potter: Who Wins and Why

We drop the One Ring on Hogwarts and test whether Harry, Dumbledore and the Hogwarts crew can walk Frodo's whole road to Mount Doom.

Harry's crew destroys the Ring, but mercy, not magic, finishes the job.

Every NerdScience fight

The Shire to Moria

A

The crew survives, but Dumbledore falls on the bridgeHarry Potter's crew vs the Nazgûl and the Balrog

The Ring works on Harry before he ever puts it on, and Dumbledore knows how. In Half-Blood Prince he slipped on Marvolo Gaunt's cursed ring because he wanted to see his dead sister. It blackened his hand and left him about a year to live. Rings get you by want, not force. Harry's 16 years with a piece of Voldemort's soul are no armor against that.

On Weathertop, the Ringwraiths attack. Dumbledore can't help, because Saruman has him locked on top of Orthanc until the eagle Gwaihir plucks him off. Harry tries Expecto Patronum and the lead wraith walks straight through his stag. A Patronus is only documented against dementors and lethifolds. In the panic the Ring ends up on Harry's finger, as it does with Frodo on this hill. The Witch-king can now see him and puts a Morgul blade in his shoulder.

What works is fire, the same thing Aragorn uses in the book to drive the wraiths off. Hermione doesn't need Fiendfyre, the fire that killed Vincent Crabbe. She has conjured flames since first year, and a wall of them makes the wraiths pull back. The wound stays. A splinter crawls toward Harry's heart, and dittany can stop scars but can't undo what dark magic takes (ask George Weasley's ear). Hermione's potions buy 17 days, until the elves of Rivendell cut it out. She Apparates nowhere afterward, because nobody has seen Mordor to picture it, so the crew walks over 1,000 miles.

Under the mountain, the Watcher in the Water grabs Luna's ankle. Luna calls out which tentacle to cut and Neville's severing charm does the job Sam does in the book. Then comes the Balrog, a fallen angel of the same order as Gandalf. On the bridge of Khazad-dûm, Dumbledore's shield stops its flaming sword. He blasts the bridge out from under it, and its whip drags him over the edge. His Elder Wand is pointed down into the dark as he falls.

We give the crew an A. Harry is the only one badly hurt, and the elves fix him. It isn't an S because losing your best wizard in chapter 1 is a heavy bill. The crew is leaderless and the Ring gets heavier every day.

Lothlórien to Amon Hen

B

The Fellowship breaks up, but nobody dies on the crewThe Fellowship vs the Ring's temptation, Boromir and the Uruk-hai

In Lothlórien, Galadriel offers Harry her mirror. People confuse it with the Mirror of Erised, but Tolkien's mirror shows things that were, are and may be, and even the wise can't always tell which. What tests desire is Galadriel herself. In the book, each traveler feels offered a choice between the quest and something they badly want. Harry has been here before. At 11 the Mirror of Erised showed him his dead parents, and Dumbledore had to move it. Now nobody can.

Ron and Hermione crack. Ron sees himself as the hero everybody cheers, and Hermione sees herself proven right with the whole world watching. Galadriel gives Harry the Phial of starlight, and he will need it. Below Amon Hen, Boromir corners Harry and pitches the Ring as a way to save Gondor. The pitch turns into a lunge. Harry has a wand, and disarming people is his signature move. He never uses it, because the Ring chooses first and he vanishes up the hill into the gaze of Sauron's Eye.

Ron reaches him because he knows how a cursed object talks. On the Horcrux hunt he wore Slytherin's locket in shifts for weeks. He calls up the hill to a friend he can't see, telling him to take it off, until Harry does. Then the Uruk-hai hit. Stunners work on them, since in canon it's giants and trolls that shrug stunners off. The problem is arithmetic. Hermione's shield charm stops physical objects, which matters against war-bow arrows that travel well over 100 mph, until a second group hits the flank.

Boromir dies there, taking arrow after arrow while standing over Neville and Luna. It is the same redemption he gets in the book, and it isn't enough. The Uruk-hai grab the two and run. The Ring is pulling Harry east, and while he stays his friends are targets. So he and Ron take a boat for Mordor, and Hermione and Hagrid chase the captives.

We put the crew at a B. Nobody dies, which counts for a lot. But Neville and Luna are prisoners, Hermione and Hagrid are hunting through hostile country, and Harry has just shown the Eye where to look.

Helm's Deep and Isengard

A

The crew wins: Rohan holds, Isengard falls, and Saruman is locked in his towerDumbledore and the Rohan-Ent alliance vs Saruman and the Uruk-hai

Neville and Luna get out first. Éomer's éored of Rohan riders hits the Uruk-hai at the edge of Fangorn, and the two crawl into the trees, where Neville treats Treebeard like a mandrake: respect and a little fear. Hermione and Hagrid follow the trail into the forest and meet a figure with a long silver beard. It's Dumbledore, and he isn't brought back from the dead. In Harry Potter no spell reawakens the dead. Gandalf returns only because Tolkien's creator god sends his angelic spirit back. Dumbledore never died.

The mechanism is the Elder Wand pointed down. In Prisoner of Azkaban, Dumbledore slowed Harry's fall off a broom, and he does it to himself here. In the book the Balrog's fall ends in deep water, and water puts out its fire. Heating a pound of water from freezing to boiling takes about 180 BTUs. Boiling it away takes 970 more, over five times as much. The whip still holds his legs, and anything holding you comes along when you Apparate, as Yaxley found when he grabbed Hermione. Dumbledore fires the release jinx Harry used on the Grindylow in Goblet of Fire, which gives off boiling water underwater, and the grip opens. He Apparates to Rivendell, where the elves spend weeks on his burns. He didn't kill the Balrog and comes back burned, not upgraded.

His first job is Théoden. The book is creepier than the films: no possession, just Wormtongue telling an old man every day that he's finished. Hermione clocks it fast because she watched Voldemort run the Ministry through a puppet minister. Dumbledore walks the king into daylight and puts his sword back in his hand. The Ents break Isengard's walls and flood Saruman's fires. At Helm's Deep, Hermione runs the wall against a host of Uruk-hai (10,000 in the film). Hagrid, 11 and a half feet tall and very hard to stun, shoves ladders off with Uruk-hai still on them. The wall falls only when Saruman's engineers light a culvert charge, which Tom Shippey calls a kind of gunpowder. Black powder is a low explosive that needs confinement, and a drain under a stone wall provides it. The defenders hold the keep all night. At first light Dumbledore arrives with the riders, and a forest that wasn't there yesterday blocks the valley.

At Orthanc, Saruman doesn't fight. He talks. He is the same kind of being as Gandalf and has already beaten Dumbledore once, locking him on the tower in a straight duel. Gandalf breaks Saruman's staff in the book as the returned head of the Wizards' Order, and Dumbledore has no such authority. What he has is history. Saruman's pitch is power used for everyone's good, the same idea a teenage Albus once wrote to Grindelwald. Hagrid starts nodding and Dumbledore hesitates. Then it fails, because Dumbledore refused the post of Minister for Magic at least three times on the grounds that he couldn't be trusted with power. The voice has nothing to grab. He says no and leaves Saruman in a tower he can't leave.

We give the crew an A, not an S. Everybody lives, Théoden is free, Isengard falls and the keep holds. But the wall fell, the relief arrived with nothing to spare, and Saruman is locked in, not beaten.

Mount Doom and the final choice

A

The crew wins: the Ring is destroyed, thanks to Harry's mercy toward GollumHarry and Ron vs Shelob, the Witch-king and the Ring itself

Harry and Ron catch Gollum, who swears on the Ring to lead them to Mordor. Harry knows a liar when he sees one, but he knows what a dark object does to a person, so he spares him. That mercy pays off later. Gollum leads them to Cirith Ungol, where Shelob's hide turns blades. Harry's Lumos makes her flinch, because her eyes are her one soft spot. Ron's Patronus, a silver terrier, charges her face. It can't hurt her, but it buys seconds. That's enough time for Galadriel's Phial to flood the tunnel with starlight, and Harry's severing charm cuts the web ahead.

In the west, the crew reaches Minas Tirith as the Witch-king drops out of the sky on his fell beast in front of Hermione. She has her Rivendell notebook, which holds Glorfindel's old prophecy that no man would kill him. She is a witch. She opens with Reducto, which blasts solid objects apart, and the fell beast's head comes off, as it does under Éowyn's sword in the book. Her shield charm holds the first mace blow and shatters on the second. It breaks her arm, Éowyn's exact injury, and the black breath creeps into her chest.

The prophecy says no man will kill him. It never says a witch will. In the book what unmakes him is Merry's dagger, forged long ago with spells against this enemy, and Tolkien says no other blade could have done it. Reducto can wreck what Hermione can see, his crown and armor, but not the spell holding the rest of him together. She blasts the crown and armor apart and the mantle falls empty. In Rivendell, after the flood at the Ford of Bruinen, Gandalf says the wraiths aren't so easily destroyed. Hermione wins the field, not the kill.

Dumbledore marches the army to the Black Gate to keep the Eye off Harry, a very Harry Potter plan. Ron half carries Harry up Mount Doom. Harry was famously bad at Occlumency, so it's Ron, not any mental discipline, who gets him up the mountain. At the edge the Ring shows him what the Mirror of Erised showed him at 11. His hand goes to his scar out of habit. Nothing, because it has been quiet since Voldemort's piece of soul died in the Forbidden Forest. The voice is Harry's own wanting. He freezes, and that is no knock on him: the Ring's pressure at that moment is more than anyone can resist, and Frodo couldn't either.

So it comes down to mercy. Gollum lunges, bites off Harry's finger, and falls into the fire with the Ring. Any gold ring dies in there, since gold melts at about 1,950°F and basaltic lava runs up to about 2,200. But this Ring shrugs off dragon fire, and only the fire that made it can end it. It does, and the Nazgûl, bound to its power, burn out with it, the Witch-king included. Harry blacks out, and the eagles, led by Gwaihir, carry both boys out. The 16 years got Harry to the edge, but they couldn't make him let go.

This chapter gets an A. The Ring is gone, Harry and Ron survive, and Hermione wins her fight. It isn't an S, because Harry is maimed, Hermione's arm is broken, and the Black Gate held on borrowed seconds. Added up, the four chapters average to an A.

What it comes down to

The crew wins because whenever raw power ran out, somebody used the canon. Fire works on the wraiths, starlight works on the spider, and a flat no beats Saruman. Harry's mercy toward Gollum finishes the Ring, since his strength couldn't. The result is an A overall.
